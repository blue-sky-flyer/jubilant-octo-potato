# Open Questions — the analytical agenda

*Owner-defined. The daily agent updates the **Current read** and **Evidence log** of each question every run, never the question itself. New questions: owner adds them here. Retired questions: owner moves them to the bottom with a one-line resolution.*

**Rules for the agent:** For each question, state a current read with a confidence (low / medium / high), cite the newest dated evidence for and against, and name what would change the read. A question with no new evidence today keeps yesterday's read and its date. Never pad. The briefing leads with the two or three questions where the read moved.

---

## Q1. How much oil and product is actually leaving the Gulf, and by what route?
*AIS transit counts at Hormuz are a floor, not a measurement: escorted or dark transits do not appear. Reconcile visible transits against arrivals at destination ports (Sikka, Vadinar, Ningbo, Qingdao, Ulsan), loadings at Fujairah and Yanbu, and exporter-country tanker export tonnage. Divergence between arrivals and visible transits is the dark-flow signal.*

**Current read:** Two true things coexist: AIS-visible Hormuz tanker tonnage is down 99% vs 2025 and the inside-strait terminals load at a tenth of normal, while Kpler puts non-Iran Gulf exports at 24.6 mb/d — above the ~23.3 mb/d pre-war baseline. The bridge is a short-haul shuttle fleet feeding Fujairah plus ship-to-ship transfer, which the terminal-call record cannot see. Confidence medium-high; read changed 2026-10-24.
**Evidence log:**
- 2026-10-24: PortWatch week to Oct 18 — Hormuz 3.7 transits/day, 0.6 tankers, **13k DWT/day vs 1.84M in 2025 (−99%)**, −48% w/w. Week to Oct 16 terminals: Ras Tanura 54 kt / 5 calls (779 kt / 11 in 2025); Ras Laffan 181 vs 1,336; Jebel Ali 224 vs 2,251; Fujairah 686 kt on 40 tanker calls (1,113 kt / 74 in 2025); Yanbu 54 kt, 0 tanker calls (PortWatch, agent arithmetic).
- 2026-10-21: Kpler — Gulf producers excluding Iran averaged **24.6 mb/d in the week to Oct 18 vs ~23.3 mb/d pre-war**, via Red Sea facilities and offshore STS, while direct Hormuz shipments remain more than a third below pre-war ([Oil & Gas 360](https://www.oilandgas360.com/middle-east-oil-exports-recover-but-shipping-risks-keep-crude-markets-volatile/)). Maritime Executive describes a **"Hormuz Shuttle Service"** of short-haul tankers carrying Gulf crude to Fujairah for transfer, with several hundred ships engaged ([Maritime Executive](https://maritime-executive.com/article/report-despite-iran-s-attacks-gulf-oil-exports-exceed-prewar-levels)).
- 2026-10-23: Brent settles $116.72 (+3.14%), six-month high, +6% on the week; WTI $103.27; futures price Brent above $100 through April; US pump average $4.29/gal, diesel $6.31 ([Sharjah24/WAM Oct 24](https://sharjah24.ae/en/Articles/2026/10/24/Oil-rises-more-than-3-to-six-month-high); [NYT Oct 23](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/23/business/energy-environment/oil-prices-iran-war.html)).
- 2026-10-23: Saudi Arabia pumped 8.3 mb/d in September per the IEA, **2.18 mb/d below its OPEC+ target**, worth ~$249m/day foregone at current prices; the East–West pipeline restarted Sep 22 but is weeks from full throughput ([House of Saud](https://houseofsaud.com/brent-114-saudi-oil-paradox)) — single-outlet.
- 2026-10-19: JINSA — Iran concentrates fire on vessels conducting ship-to-ship transfers, "now responsible for most of the crude still crossing Hormuz"; 26 attacks on ships in October vs 13 in all of September ([JINSA](https://jinsa.org/jinsa_report/iran-escalates-before-midterm-elections)).
**Would change the read:** Fujairah tanker calls falling below ~30 in a week, or a Kpler print showing Gulf exports back below pre-war while terminal loadings stay suppressed.

## Q2. What is really happening inside Iran: goods, borders, rail, people?
*Below the social-media layer. Border crossings and truck traffic at Bazargan/Gürbulak (Turkey), Shalamcheh and Mehran (Iraq), Mirjaveh/Taftan (Pakistan), Islam Qala/Dogharoun (Afghanistan), Astara (Azerbaijan), Sarakhs/Incheh Borun (Turkmenistan). Port calls at Caspian ports (Anzali, Amirabad, Nowshahr) versus Gulf ports (Shahid Rajaee, Bushehr, Khomeini). Rail: INSTC, Iran–Turkey, Iran–Turkmenistan. People: outbound flows to Turkey, Armenia, Iraq, Pakistan; internal displacement; flight schedules at IKA and Mashhad.*

**Current read:** The overland squeeze has become a coordinated, US-directed land blockade in all but name: Pakistan reversed its reopening citing sanctions compliance, Iraq shut Sheeb indefinitely under reported US pressure, and ~17,000 Iranian trucks are stranded at Pakistani borders per the Tehran Chamber of Commerce. With sea routes carrying 75–80% of Iran's trade pre-blockade, the land substitute is now being closed faster than it can be used. Confidence high on the closures, medium on the coordination. Read changed 2026-10-24.
**Evidence log:**
- 2026-10-24: Pakistan reversed its decision to allow trade at **Taftan and Gabd–Rimdan**, citing compliance with US sanctions, security and smuggling; ~3,000 trucks wait at Rimdan alone with rice, fruit and LPG; Tehran Chamber of Commerce puts **~17,000 Iranian trucks stranded** at Pakistani borders; an Iranian official says exports to Pakistan have stopped ([Kurdistan24/WSJ](https://www.kurdistan24.net/en/story/948152/pakistan-border-closures-deepen-iran-trade-strain-as-food-prices-surge)). Pakistan LPG Association reports availability tightening after Iranian imports were interrupted ([Investify/Business Recorder](https://www.investify.pk/news/100000158766/lpg-shortage-worsens-after-supply-issues)).
- 2026-10-22: Iran International — Pezeshkian says the maritime blockade halted trade through southern sea routes, previously **75–80% of Iran's trade**; SNSC secretary Rezaei concedes a prolonged blockade means "families will pay the price"; AFP reports the US pressing Iran's neighbours to close crossings; Turkish freight traffic still disrupted ([Iran International](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202610228890)).
- 2026-10-21: Iraq closed the **Sheeb/Chazabeh** crossing to passengers, vehicles and trucks until further notice; Al Jazeera cites an Iraqi official saying PM Zaidi ordered it shut indefinitely with others to follow; AFP cites two Iraqi sources saying Washington pressed Baghdad to leave only two crossings open ([Iran International](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202610219901); [JPost Oct 22](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/iran-news/article-912385)). - 2026-10-24: PortWatch week to Oct 16 — Iran split **Caspian 41 vs Gulf 35** (2025: 24 vs 47), the northern pivot restored; Bandar Shahid Rajaee exports collapse 257 → **43 kt** w/w; Anzali calls 6 → 17, Amirabad 11 → 17; Kharg zero exports for a **seventh consecutive week** (PortWatch, agent arithmetic).
- 2026-10-21: Trade press reports severe congestion at **Bazargan–Gürbulak and Sero–Esendere**, with waits of 12 to 25 days on the Iranian side in October 2026 ([Manis Steel export-logistics page](https://manissteel.com/en/export-logistics)) — commercial source, single-sourced, logged as corroboration of the Turkish-corridor disruption only.
**Would change the read:** A dated reopening of Taftan or Sheeb, or truck-count data from Bazargan/Gürbulak or Astara showing throughput absorbing the diverted volume.
