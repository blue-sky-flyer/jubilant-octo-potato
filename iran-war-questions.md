# Open Questions — the analytical agenda

*Owner-defined. The daily agent updates the **Current read** and **Evidence log** of each question every run, never the question itself. New questions: owner adds them here. Retired questions: owner moves them to the bottom with a one-line resolution.*

**Rules for the agent:** For each question, state a current read with a confidence (low / medium / high), cite the newest dated evidence for and against, and name what would change the read. A question with no new evidence today keeps yesterday's read and its date. Never pad. The briefing leads with the two or three questions where the read moved.

## Q1. How much oil and product is actually leaving the Gulf, and by what route?
*AIS transit counts at Hormuz are a floor, not a measurement: escorted or dark transits do not appear. Reconcile visible transits against arrivals at destination ports (Sikka, Vadinar, Ningbo, Qingdao, Ulsan), loadings at Fujairah and Yanbu, and exporter-country tanker export tonnage. Divergence between arrivals and visible transits is the dark-flow signal.*

**Current read:** The dark-flow gap is now measurable and roughly fiftyfold: inside-Gulf terminal loadings of 1,071 kt in the week to Nov 6 imply ≥150k DWT/day must cross the strait against 3k AIS-visible, and the average visible "tanker" at 3,000 DWT is coastal craft, not a carrier. Fujairah's 24.9 kt per call against 13.1 in 2025 is the STS-aggregation signature. Confidence high; read changed 2026-11-13.
**Evidence log:**
- 2026-11-13: PortWatch week to Nov 6 — Ras Tanura 372 kt, Ras Laffan 425, Jebel Ali 274 = **1,071 kt vs 629 prior week (+70%)**, 21% of 2025's 5,110 kt; Hormuz tanker tonnage **−78% to 3k DWT/day**; Fujairah 997 kt on 40 calls vs 893 kt on 68 in 2025 (PortWatch, agent arithmetic).
- 2026-11-14: PortWatch not available this run — API quota exceeded (HTTP 429). No chokepoint or port series collected; the Nov 6 window remains the last observation (PortWatch).
- 2026-10-21: Kpler — Gulf producers excluding Iran averaged **24.6 mb/d in the week to Oct 18 vs ~23.3 mb/d pre-war**, via Red Sea facilities and offshore STS ([Oil & Gas 360](https://www.oilandgas360.com/middle-east-oil-exports-recover-but-shipping-risks-keep-crude-markets-volatile/)); Maritime Executive describes a **"Hormuz Shuttle Service"** of short-haul tankers to Fujairah ([Maritime Executive](https://maritime-executive.com/article/report-despite-iran-s-attacks-gulf-oil-exports-exceed-prewar-levels)).
- 2026-11-10: Lloyd's List Intelligence puts Hormuz traffic at roughly **20% of the 138 vessels/day pre-war baseline**; JMIC Nov 9 advisory holds the strait threat assessment at SEVERE ([House of Saud Nov 10](https://houseofsaud.com/irgc-tankers-hormuz-nov10)) — single carrier.
- 2026-10-24: PortWatch week to Oct 18 — Hormuz 3.7 transits/day, 0.6 tankers, **13k DWT/day vs 1.84M in 2025 (−99%)**; Fujairah 686 kt on 40 tanker calls vs 1,113 kt / 74 in 2025 (PortWatch, agent arithmetic).
**Would change the read:** Fujairah tanker calls falling below ~30 in a week, or a Kpler print showing Gulf exports back below pre-war while terminal loadings stay suppressed.

## Q2. What is really happening inside Iran: goods, borders, rail, people?
*Below the social-media layer. Border crossings and truck traffic at Bazargan/Gürbulak (Turkey), Shalamcheh and Mehran (Iraq), Mirjaveh/Taftan (Pakistan), Islam Qala/Dogharoun (Afghanistan), Astara (Azerbaijan), Sarakhs/Incheh Borun (Turkmenistan). Port calls at Caspian ports (Anzali, Amirabad, Nowshahr) versus Gulf ports (Shahid Rajaee, Bushehr, Khomeini). Rail: INSTC, Iran–Turkey, Iran–Turkmenistan. People: outbound flows to Turkey, Armenia, Iraq, Pakistan; internal displacement; flight schedules at IKA and Mashhad.*

**Current read:** The land blockade is being formalised institutionally (Task Force Iron Gate) at the same time as its physical enforcement is loosening at the margin — Taftan reopened partially on Nov 9 and Sheeb on Nov 10, while Bazargan runs a five-day truck queue. The binding constraint inside Iran is now payments and purchasing power, not only physical closure. Confidence medium-high; read unchanged since 2026-11-12.
**Evidence log:**
- 2026-11-13: TV7 reports Turkey, Pakistan, Iraq and other Iranian neighbours pushing back on American demands to close their border crossings ([TV7 Israel Nov 13](https://www.tv7israelnews.com/vod/series/563?episode-id=KrCfed6NALY)) — single carrier, directionally consistent with the Nov 9–10 reopenings.
- 2026-11-11: Taftan reopened on a partial basis Monday with thousands of trucks still waiting on the Pakistani side ([Skardu Times Nov 11](https://skardutimes.com/news/taftan-crossing-reopens-as-partial-iran-trade-resumes-e004f7)); Iraq reopened Al-Sheeb on Nov 10 and closed Mandali on Nov 13 for maintenance ([Shafaq Nov 9](https://shafaq.com/en/society/Iraq-to-reopen-Al-Sheeb-crossing-with-Iran)).
- 2026-11-11: ILNA reports workers in Tehran advertising kidneys, liver tissue and bone marrow for sale; a parliamentary committee chair says **about eight million people have fallen into absolute poverty** ([Iran International Nov 11](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202611118553)).
- 2026-11-08: Maritime Executive, from Google Earth imagery, documents a **five-day truck queue at Bazargan**, and states the naval blockade is shutting out **80% of trade that previously entered Iran by ship**, principally through Bandar Abbas ([Maritime Executive Nov 8](https://maritime-executive.com/article/centcom-mirrors-naval-blockade-with-iron-gate-for-iran-s-borders)).
- 2026-11-07: CENTCOM establishes **Task Force Iron Gate** to disrupt land and Caspian transit, ~20 staff in Tampa ([Reuters Nov 7](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/expanding-naval-blockade-us-military-looks-restrict-iranian-land-commerce-2026-11-07)); the same investigation finds Iran imported **at least $2bn** in hardware since January 2025 via the Caspian, Central Asian trucking and Chinese freight aircraft.
**Would change the read:** Truck-count or throughput data from Bazargan or Astara showing the diverted volume being absorbed, or a re-closure of Taftan or Sheeb.
