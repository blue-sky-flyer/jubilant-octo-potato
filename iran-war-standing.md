## Strategic Reserve Countdown — US Endurance Ceiling (Day 0)
*Last materially changed: 2026-09-12 (IEA Sep 11 OMR: inventories "shrinking," supply gap widens, full recovery pushed to 2027; Saudi East-West pipeline shutdown eliminates Saudi bypass route; Bab el-Mandeb near-zero transit adds to supply constraint; dual-chokepoint near-collapse is the most severe supply-side event of the conflict)*

**One-line arithmetic (updated Sep 12):** SPR **286.6M bbl** (EIA w/e Aug 28, GEF Sep 10) minus political floor ~200M bbl = ~86.6M bbl usable buffer. At the **3.7M bbl weekly draw** rate, political floor arrives approximately **~23 weeks → Day 0 approximately early-to-mid February 2027**. At a 3.1M bbl/week pace (two-week trend Aug 14–28), Day 0 slides to ~early March 2027. **Operative range: early January – early March 2027.** *(Low-to-moderate confidence on precise timing.)*

**Sep 12 update — IEA OMR, Saudi pipeline shutdown, dual-chokepoint collapse:**

- **IEA Sep 11 OMR — most explicit inventory warning of conflict ([Reuters Sep 11](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/global-2026-oil-supply-gap-deepen-delayed-return-normal-gulf-flows-iea-says-2026-09-11)):** "Inventories have so far played a crucial role in balancing the market. With buffers shrinking and the global refining system stretched to the limit, the need for progress in resolving the conflict in the Middle East is greater than ever to avoid further market tightening." Full-year supply gap widens beyond previous forecast; return of normal Middle East flows pushed to 2027. EIA simultaneously raised 2027 US crude production forecast to 14.3 mb/d — a 2027 relief signal that does not help 2026 inventory trajectory.

- **Saudi East-West pipeline shutdown (Sep 12):** Carries ~1.2–2M bpd of Saudi crude to Yanbu (Red Sea) bypassing Hormuz. Its precautionary shutdown — triggered by an Iraq-originating drone attack — eliminates the primary Saudi bypass route at the exact moment Bab el-Mandeb is at near-zero transit. For SPR arithmetic: if Saudi crude reaching non-Hormuz markets falls, global supply tightens further, accelerating the draw rate. The shutdown duration is unknown; Saudi Arabia has not confirmed damage vs. precautionary-only closure.

- **Dual-chokepoint near-collapse (Sep 12):** Hormuz at 2 vessels/day; Bab el-Mandeb at 1 vessel/day (Kpler, JPost/Reuters Sep 12). Both of the world's primary Persian Gulf oil export chokepoints are simultaneously at near-zero — the first such event of the conflict. Combined with Saudi East-West pipeline shutdown, global supply tightening is accelerating faster than any prior point in the siege.

- **SPR Day 0 clock implications:** The IEA's explicit "buffers shrinking" warning, combined with dual-chokepoint near-collapse and Saudi bypass route shutdown, suggests the operative Day 0 range (early Jan – early Mar 2027) may compress toward the earlier end if the current supply constraint persists. The Oman diplomatic channel (GCC-Iran, Sep 12) is the only near-term offset; a "temporary arrangement" for Hormuz transit management would be the most consequential single event for slowing the SPR draw rate.

**Prior data points (carried):**
- Commercial crude BUILD of 17.422M bbl (GEF Sep 10, w/e Aug 28) — demand-destruction signal; does not alter strategic draw trajectory.
- Salt cavern structural risk (Fortune Aug 17): drawdown threatens physical damage to 60 underground caverns; DOE safe operating minimum ~70M bbl (⚠️ directionally confirmed).
- IEA August OMR: 2026 demand −1.6 mb/d; global supply 101.5 mb/d in July, 6.3 mb/d below year-ago; 8.3 mb/d Gulf output still shut in; full-year supply shortfall 4.3 mb/d. 290M bbl released by IEA members since Mar 11.

- EIA STEO Aug 11: full-year 2026 Brent $87/bbl — materially exceeded at $104.42 Sep 11.

This resolves the Sep 11 single-sourced Mocha seizure with double-sourcing and adds the Mayun Island dimension — Houthis now control both the Yemeni mainland Red Sea coast AND the island flanking the strait's mouth.

Houthi control of the island gives shore-based missile and drone launch capability directly over the strait's full breadth — a qualitatively different interdiction capability from prior inland-launched attacks. Combined with mainland coast control, the Houthis now hold a dual-bank chokepoint-control position unprecedented in this conflict or its 2024-25 predecessor.

- **Houthi spokesman Saree statement:** "Maritime navigation is safe for all companies except for Saudi vessels." Markets are not treating this assurance as credible given the Mayun seizure and prior pattern of Houthi unpredictability.

- **Saudi East-West crude pipeline shutdown (Sep 12):** Saudi Arabia shut its East-West crude pipeline as a precaution after it came under attack ([Al Jazeera Sep 12](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/liveblog/2026/9/12/iran-war-live-houthis-control-red-sea-coast-saudi-pipeline-shut-down)). The pipeline carries crude from eastern Saudi fields to Yanbu (Red Sea) — Saudi Arabia's primary route bypassing Hormuz. Capacity: ~2M bpd. Its shutdown, combined with Bab el-Mandeb near-zero transit, means Saudi Arabia currently has no functional export route for its oil.

- **IRGC officers in Yemen guiding Houthi advance (US-official assessment, Sep 10):** CNN/US officials confirm hundreds of IRGC officers currently in Yemen working with Houthis to shut down Bab el-Mandeb ([Hayman/Facebook Sep 10](https://www.facebook.com/felix.hayman/posts/september-10-2026-thursdaywe-woke-up-today-to-news-that-yemens-iran-backed-houth/10164088774921785)). ⚠️ Single-sourced at US-official attribution level; consistent with established IRGC-Houthi command relationship.

Houthi Mayun seizure closes the Red Sea/Suez route for vessels currently using it, forcing another wave onto the Cape route — compounding global shipping costs independent of Hormuz dynamics. Vessels already on Cape route are unaffected.
