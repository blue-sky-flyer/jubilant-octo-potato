## Strategic Reserve Countdown — US Endurance Ceiling (Day 0)
*Last materially changed: 2026-08-27 (SPR confirmed 289.7M bbl per EIA w/e Aug 21, GEF updated Aug 27 12:30 UTC — 3.7M bbl weekly draw; lowest since December 1982; Day 0 clock updated)*

**One-line arithmetic (updated Aug 27):** SPR **289.7M bbl** (EIA w/e Aug 21, GEF updated Aug 27 12:30 UTC — confirmed as lowest since December 1982; Energy Factbook Aug 28 corroborates: "lowest level since November 26, 1982") minus political floor ~200M bbl = ~89.7M bbl usable buffer. At the **3.7M bbl weekly draw** observed in the week ending Aug 21, the political floor arrives in approximately **~24 weeks → Day 0 approximately mid-February 2027** at that rate. However, the two-week average from w/e Aug 7 (298.7M bbl) to w/e Aug 21 (289.7M bbl) is 4.5M bbl/week, implying **~20 weeks → early January 2027**. At the prior longer-run ~2.7M bbl/week average, Day 0 would arrive ~mid-May 2027. The operative range is now **early January – mid-February 2027** at current draw rates. *(Low-to-moderate confidence on precise timing.)*

**Sep 5 forward-looking note:** EIA STEO releases Sep 9. Brent at $95.73 (Sep 4) is ~$8–9 above the Aug 11 STEO baseline of $87/bbl, and Hormuz transits remain at 8 vessels/day (Windward Sep 3). The Sep 9 STEO is the first EIA revision window since the "tanker for tanker" doctrine was established and the Sep 1–2 ~100-target exchange occurred. Upside risk to the draw-rate estimate is elevated. The Brent forward curve is in **steep backwardation** (re-verified Sep 5, correcting prior "contango" notation) — consistent with acute physical scarcity, not demand recovery, and therefore consistent with sustained or accelerated SPR draw pressure rather than easing.

**New data point — commercial crude BUILD (GEF Aug 27):** US commercial crude inventories at approximately 428.9 million barrels — a BUILD of 17.422 million barrels, the largest weekly increase since January 2023. This is a demand-destruction signal consistent with IEA's −1.6 mb/d downgrade and does not alter the strategic draw trajectory.

DOE safe operating minimum ~70M bbl (distinct from ~200M bbl political floor). ⚠️ DOE safe-operating-minimum figure sourced via CNBC via DOE spokesperson — treat as directionally confirmed but not independently verified against published DOE technical standards.

**Supply context (carried):** IEA August OMR (Aug 8/12): 2026 demand now projected −1.6 mb/d; global supply 101.5 mb/d in July, 6.3 mb/d below year-ago; 8.3 mb/d of Gulf output still shut in; full-year supply shortfall averaging 4.3 mb/d. - **302 transits through Bab el-Mandeb** in the week of Aug 17–23 — the highest level since the Houthi Saudi-port blockade began July 20 — driven by bulk carriers, product tankers, and containerships. Of these, 257 were traceable with AIS.
- The following week (Aug 24–30): **202 traceable transits** through Bab el-Mandeb. Natural week-on-week fluctuation noted; fall attributed to reduced product tanker, containership, and bulker transits.
- **Suez Canal: 290 ships transited Aug 24–30** (on par with 294 the prior week). Still **36% below normal** but **30% higher year-on-year**. Described as "levels not seen since the start of 2024."

Ships using Bab el-Mandeb/Suez are not Gulf-exit vessels — they are non-Gulf cargo flows that had rerouted to Cape during peak Houthi activity and are now reverting.
