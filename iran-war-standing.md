## Strategic Reserve Countdown — US Endurance Ceiling (Day 0)
*Last materially changed: 2026-09-12 (IEA Sep 11 OMR: inventories "shrinking," supply gap widens, 2.5 mb/d demand contraction 2026, full recovery pushed to 2027; Saudi East-West pipeline shutdown eliminates Saudi bypass route; Bab el-Mandeb near-zero transit adds to supply constraint; dual-chokepoint near-collapse is the most severe simultaneous supply disruption of the conflict)*

**One-line arithmetic (updated Sep 12):** SPR **286.6M bbl** (EIA w/e Aug 28, GEF Sep 10) minus political floor ~200M bbl = ~86.6M bbl usable buffer. At the **3.7M bbl weekly draw** rate, political floor arrives approximately **~23 weeks → Day 0 approximately early-to-mid February 2027**. At a 3.1M bbl/week pace (two-week trend Aug 14–28), Day 0 slides to ~early March 2027. **Operative range: early January – early March 2027.** *(Low-to-moderate confidence on precise timing.)*

**Sep 12 update — IEA OMR, Saudi pipeline shutdown, dual-chokepoint collapse:**

- **IEA Sep 11 OMR — most explicit inventory warning of conflict; 2.5 mb/d demand contraction confirmed ([Reuters Sep 11](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/global-2026-oil-supply-gap-deepen-delayed-return-normal-gulf-flows-iea-says-2026-09-11); [TradingEconomics Sep 12](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/brent-crude-oil) — double-sourced):** "Inventories have so far played a crucial role in balancing the market. With buffers shrinking and the global refining system stretched to the limit, the need for progress in resolving the conflict in the Middle East is greater than ever to avoid further market tightening." IEA now forecasts a **2.5 mb/d contraction in global oil demand for 2026** (TradingEconomics summary of IEA OMR). Full-year supply gap widens beyond previous forecast; return of normal Middle East flows pushed to 2027. EIA simultaneously raised 2027 US crude production forecast to 14.3 mb/d — a 2027 relief signal that does not help 2026 inventory trajectory.

- **China crude import recovery signal (Sep 12, new):** IEA Sep OMR notes "China is on track to import roughly the same volume of crude in September as in August, with purchases recovering from June's decade-low" (TradingEconomics summary). ⚠️ Single-sourced (TradingEconomics/IEA); not independently confirmed by Kpler/Vortexa in pack. If confirmed, this would be the first demand-side positive for global supply-demand balance since the siege phase began — partially offsetting the supply-side deterioration from dual-chokepoint collapse.

- **Saudi East-West pipeline shutdown (Sep 12):** Carries ~2M bpd of Saudi crude to Yanbu (Red Sea) bypassing Hormuz. Its precautionary shutdown eliminates the primary Saudi bypass route at the exact moment Bab el-Mandeb is at near-zero transit. For SPR arithmetic: if Saudi crude reaching non-Hormuz markets falls, global supply tightens further, accelerating draw rate. Duration unknown; Saudi Arabia has not confirmed damage vs. precautionary-only closure.

- **Dual-chokepoint near-collapse (Sep 12):** Hormuz at 2 vessels/day; Bab el-Mandeb at 1 vessel/day (Kpler, JPost/Reuters Sep 12). Both primary Persian Gulf oil export chokepoints simultaneously at near-zero. Combined with Saudi East-West pipeline shutdown, global supply tightening is accelerating faster than any prior point in the siege.

- **SPR Day 0 clock implications:** The IEA's 2.5 mb/d demand contraction is an important moderating factor — lower demand globally means the SPR draw rate may slow even as supply tightens, because refiners are processing less crude. This creates a tension between supply-side tightening (faster draw) and demand-side destruction (slower draw). The Oman diplomatic channel (Araghchi in Muscat) remains the primary variable: even a temporary Hormuz arrangement would be the single most consequential near-term relief event for slowing the SPR draw rate.

**Prior data points (carried):**
- Commercial crude BUILD of 17.422M bbl (GEF Sep 10, w/e Aug 28) — demand-destruction signal; does not alter strategic draw trajectory.
- Salt cavern structural risk (Fortune Aug 17): drawdown threatens physical damage to 60 underground caverns; DOE safe operating minimum ~70M bbl (⚠️ directionally confirmed).
- IEA August OMR: 2026 demand −1.6 mb/d; global supply 101.5 mb/d in July, 6.3 mb/d below year-ago; 8.3 mb/d Gulf output still shut in; full-year supply shortfall 4.3 mb/d. 290M bbl released by IEA members since Mar 11.
- OPEC August report: global oil demand growth 600K bpd in 2026 (−25% vs July forecast).
- EIA STEO Aug 11: full-year 2026 Brent $87/bbl — materially exceeded at $104.61 Sep 11.
- EIA STEO Sep 9 released: results not yet confirmed in pack.
