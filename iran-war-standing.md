## Strategic Reserve Countdown — US Endurance Ceiling (Day 0)
*Last materially changed: 2026-09-14 (Hormuz talks postponement removes primary near-term SPR relief variable; Brent $107.56 four-month high accelerates draw-rate pressure on importers; Saudi three-pathway export blockage continuing with pipeline shutdown of indeterminate duration)*

**One-line arithmetic (updated Sep 14):** SPR **286.6M bbl** (EIA w/e Aug 28, GEF Sep 10) minus political floor ~200M bbl = ~86.6M bbl usable buffer. At the **3.7M bbl weekly draw** rate, political floor arrives approximately **~23 weeks → Day 0 approximately early-to-mid February 2027**. At a 3.1M bbl/week pace (two-week trend Aug 14–28), Day 0 slides to ~early March 2027. **Operative range: early January – early March 2027.** *(Low-to-moderate confidence on precise timing.)*

**Sep 14 update — Hormuz talks postponement; Brent $107.56; Saudi three-pathway blockage continuing:**

- **Hormuz talks postponement removes the primary near-term SPR relief variable (Sep 14, updated):** The Monday Sep 14 GCC-Iran meeting — identified in the Sep 12 briefing as the nearest-term binary for the SPR clock — did not occur and was explicitly postponed (TradingEconomics Sep 13; ILTV Sep 14). With no rescheduled date confirmed, the SPR clock's next identifiable relief event is now indeterminate. A successful "temporary arrangement" would have been the single most consequential near-term relief event for the SPR draw rate; that relief pathway is now removed from the near-term window. The operative Day 0 range (early January – early March 2027) tightens toward the earlier end of the range as a result.

- **Brent $107.56 — four-month high sustaining maximum import-bill pressure (Sep 13 confirmed):** At Brent $107.56 — up 21.51% over the past month (TradingEconomics Sep 13) — global refiners face maximum crude input costs. The EIA STEO Aug 11 baseline of $87/bbl full-year 2026 has been materially exceeded for multiple consecutive weeks. Higher Brent accelerates the rate at which importing nations draw on strategic reserves to buffer price impacts. The IEA Sep 11 OMR warning ("buffers shrinking, refining system stretched to the limit") was issued when Brent was at $105-106; the $107.56 Sep 13 close compounds that warning.

- **Saudi three-pathway export blockage continuing (Sep 14, carried/updated):** (1) Hormuz — ~2 vessels/day; (2) Bab el-Mandeb — ~1 vessel/day, Houthis hold Mayun Island; (3) East-West pipeline to Yanbu — shutdown, Iraq-originating attack confirmed (AJ Sep 12). Houthi King Khalid Air Base strike claim (JPost Sep 13) raises risk of Saudi pipeline/energy infrastructure further threatened. Duration of pipeline shutdown remains the most consequential single unknown for 2026 SPR arithmetic. A precautionary-only closure reversing within days would be less damaging; confirmed infrastructure damage would extend the export blockage by weeks to months and compress the SPR clock.

- **IEA demand contraction partially offsets supply tightening (structural, carried):** IEA Sep 11 OMR forecasts 2.5 mb/d demand contraction 2026. Demand destruction partially offsets supply tightening in SPR draw arithmetic. The IEA's simultaneous inventory-depletion warning ("buffers shrinking") indicates supply disruption is currently dominating the demand-destruction effect. This balance tips further toward supply dominance as the Hormuz talks failure removes a price-relief signal.

This is structurally more constraining than a meeting that occurs and fails: it suggests the hardliner track blocked even the attempt to negotiate. The pragmatist tipping-point clock is therefore not accelerated by this development.

- **IRGC hardliner track active simultaneously (Sep 14, updated):** Houthis claimed the King Khalid Air Base strike (JPost Sep 13); Saudi-Houthi kinetic exchange escalating to Saudi interior air infrastructure. The dual-track divergence has reached its most extreme point of the conflict: the IRGC is blocking talks while simultaneously escalating kinetically.

- **Maximum dual-side economic pressure (continuing, updated):** Brent $107.56 four-month high (Sep 13) means Iran's stranded crude exports remain maximally economically painful while global importers face maximum energy costs. Iran faces maximum economic siege (tanker fleet attrited, TGL revoked, IAEA UNSC referral, dark-fleet only). The Brent $107.56 reading confirms the IRGC's strategy of maximum chokepoint pressure has not produced an Iranian capitulation offer — and the hardliner track's blocking of talks suggests they do not intend to allow one at current terms.

**Hardliner veto confirmed dominant (updated Sep 14):** Talks blocked before occurring. Iraq pipeline attack and Bab el-Mandeb campaign confirm continued IRGC kinetic decision-making. Pragmatist track stalled at the meeting-scheduling stage for the first time since Araghchi's Muscat travel.

**Military reconstitution:** Iran **claims** >75% of pre-war missile/drone capability retained (CBS citing Iranian state sources, Aug 12 — **Iranian claim, not independent confirmation**). IRGC-guided proxy network operational in Yemen, Iraq, and Red Sea simultaneously.

**Fleet attrition (carried from Sep 10):** Eight Iranian tankers struck/sunk Sep 5–8 before Iran's Sep 9 10-vessel response. Iran's maritime offensive capacity shifting toward unmanned/asymmetric and proxy platforms.

**Trajectory:** Tipping point Q1–Q2 2027 (operative baseline, re-verified Aug 29). The Sep 14 talks postponement eliminates the nearest-term variable that could have decelerated the tipping-point clock. With the hardliner track dominant and the pragmatist track stalled, the disintegration clock advances without a near-term relief event. The IRGC's simultaneous blocking of talks and escalation of kinetic pressure (Houthi-Saudi front, Iraq pipeline) signals hardliner confidence in the economic-siege endurance posture. The resolution of this dual-track tension — pragmatist Oman engagement vs. IRGC escalation — now requires a new catalyst; no candidate catalyst is identified in today's pack.
