**Update protocol (daily agent):** For each section below, ask one question each run: *did anything material change in the last 24 hours?* If no — touch nothing here, and the briefing bullet reads "unchanged since [date]". ## Red Sea / Bab el-Mandeb — Baseline
*Last materially changed: 2026-09-23 (Aramco restarts the East-West pipeline Sep 22 and resumes Yanbu exports at low rate, targeting 4 mb/d; PortWatch confirms Yanbu at absolute zero in the outage week; Bab el-Mandeb tanker DWT deteriorates to −50% vs 2025 while Suez is at 2025 parity — the Red Sea is functioning as a terminating basin rather than a through-route)*

**Sep 23 UPDATE — the trigger fired:**

- **East-West pipeline restarted Sep 22; Yanbu exports resuming (new):** Three sources briefed on the matter told Reuters Saudi Arabia restarted the pipeline and could resume Red Sea exports from Yanbu the same day; the line was pumping at a low rate, Aramco is seeking to return to about 4 mb/d, and a security source said full resumption could take weeks ([Reuters Sep 22](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/saudi-arabia-restarts-east-west-oil-pipeline-resume-exports-yanbu-sources-say-2026-09-22)). A first tanker bound for China is reported set to load ([Herald Business Sep 22](https://mbiz.heraldcorp.com/article/10882618)). Brent fell more than $2 to its lowest since Sep 8 on the news. **This resolves the "would change the read" condition carried since Sep 20.** Yanbu is a tradeable node again; its availability is set by pumping rate, not by Houthi missiles.

- **PortWatch brackets the outage precisely (week to Sep 18, new):** Yanbu King Fahd recorded **0 calls, 0 tanker calls, 0 kt exports, 0 kt imports**, against 2 calls/48 kt the prior week and 24 calls/938 kt in the same 2025 week. The port was at absolute zero for a full week — consistent with Breakwave's "no observed crude loadings since Sep 16" and with the Sep 11–13 pipeline shutdown, and inconsistent with any claim that the Sep 19 Houthi strike on Yanbu removed incremental barrels.

- **The structural finding — the Red Sea has become a terminating basin (agent arithmetic on PortWatch, new):** Week to Sep 20: Bab el-Mandeb **24.7 transits/day, 6.6 tankers, 319k DWT/day** — tanker DWT **−50% vs 2025 and −27% week-on-week**, a clear deterioration from last week's −31%/+29%. Suez over the same week: **41.9 transits/day, 17.0 tankers, 709k DWT/day — −1% vs 2025 and +3% w/w**, effectively at pre-war-baseline parity. In the same week of 2025 the Suez:Bab el-Mandeb tanker-DWT ratio was 1.13x; it is now **2.2x**. **Inference (medium-high confidence):** roughly half the tanker tonnage entering the Suez Canal now terminates at Red Sea ports — Jeddah, Yanbu, Aqaba, Sudan — rather than continuing through Bab el-Mandeb to the Indian Ocean. The corridor is functioning as a cul-de-sac serving intra-basin trade. Two consequences: (1) Yanbu's restart loads into the Suez-side leg and can normalise without Houthi transit risk being resolved; (2) Bab el-Mandeb throughput is a weaker proxy for Suez revenue than it was pre-war, and Egypt's exposure is less severe than the BEM number alone implies.
