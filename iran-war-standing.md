# Iran War — Standing Sections
*Slow-moving sections of the daily briefing live here, in full. The daily briefing carries ONE bullet + link per section, and reprints a section in full only on the day it materially changes. Each header carries a "last materially changed" date so readers can see staleness at a glance.*

**Update protocol (daily agent):** For each section below, ask one question each run: *did anything material change in the last 24 hours?* If no — touch nothing here, and the briefing bullet reads "unchanged since [date]". ## On the Ground in Iran
*Last materially changed: 2026-09-24 (AP reports from the Gürbulak crossing: 7-km queues and dwell times up from a maximum of three days to more than five — the first hard measurement of the overland ceiling, and the trigger condition carried on Q2 since Sep 20)*

**Sep 24 UPDATE — the land border, finally measured:**

- **Gürbulak/Bazargan dwell times up roughly two-thirds (AP, Sep 24, new):** AP reporters at Turkey's largest crossing into Iran describe hundreds of trucks idling and lines stretching for miles. A driver with 15 years on the route, Muhammed Rıza Shiri, 55, waited **four days** to carry a load of rubber into Iran, at the end of a **7-kilometre** queue: "We used to wait, maximum, for three days here. Because of the war, the waiting times exceed five days." Pezeshkian has pressed to accelerate overland trade as the blockade cuts off the majority of imports ([AP Sep 24](https://apnews.com/article/iran-turkey-border-trade-trucks-delays-war-11f510a93bdf56754f994faff99b3e02)).
- **Agent arithmetic and inference (medium-high confidence):** border dwell has gone from ≤3 days to >5, i.e. **+67% or more**. On a notional 10-day round trip of which three days were waiting, a move to five-plus days lengthens the cycle to 12+ days — a **17–20% reduction in trips per truck per year** for an essentially fixed regional fleet. Because the constraint is queue time rather than road capacity, extra trucks do not relieve it; only customs throughput or new gates do. This is the quantitative form of Fortune's Sep 20 "red tape and gridlock" finding and **fires the Q2 trigger condition carried since Sep 20**.
- **Read:** both of Iran's substitution channels are now demonstrably capacity-capped rather than scaling — the Caspian at a fixed 0.9 kt per call, the land border at a fixed number of customs slots per day. That is the analytical core: further tightening of the naval blockade has low marginal economic value, while Turkish customs throughput and the two crossings under negotiation are now the highest-leverage variables in Iran's import position.

**Sep 23 UPDATE (carried) — the inversion holds, the base keeps shrinking:**

- **Port-call split, week to Sep 18 (PortWatch):** Caspian **47 calls** (Amirabad 22, Anzali 15, Nowshahr 10) against **17** across all eleven monitored Gulf ports. Same week 2025: Caspian 15, Gulf 44. The inversion is now four weeks old and deepening on the Gulf side — Gulf calls fell from 23 to 17 week-on-week, with Bandar Shahid Rajaee down from 9 calls to 2 and zero tanker calls at every Iranian Gulf port in the set.
- **Tonnage (agent arithmetic on PortWatch):** Caspian imports **43 kt on 47 calls = 0.91 kt/call**; Gulf imports **133 kt on 17 calls = 7.8 kt/call**. Total visible seaborne imports **176 kt, −24% week-on-week** (from 231 kt) and now roughly half the 2025 level. Caspian share of visible import tonnage **24%**, from 20%.
- **Read:** the efficiency ratio is unchanged — the northern route has not become more capable, the southern route has simply shrunk further. The Caspian is absorbing share by default. The trigger for a read change (Caspian tonnage above ~3 kt/call) has not fired in four weeks of data, which is itself the finding: this is a fixed-capacity substitute, and Iran's marginal import capacity is therefore set at the land borders, not at the waterline.
