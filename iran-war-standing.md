# Iran War — Standing Sections

**Update protocol (daily agent):** For each section below, ask one question each run: *did anything material change in the last 24 hours?* If no — touch nothing here, and the briefing bullet reads "unchanged since [date]". ## Red Sea / Bab el-Mandeb — Baseline
*Last materially changed: 2026-09-26 (war-risk on tankers calling Yanbu triples to ~3% of hull value with loadings still not resumed; Greek-crewed Patriots intercept a ballistic missile and drone near Yanbu; Mecca Pact chiefs of staff convene in the kingdom; ISW moves to weekend coverage of the Houthi-Saudi front)*

**Sep 26 UPDATE — the Red Sea outlet is now blocked by an insurance market, not a pipeline:**

- **Yanbu war risk triples (new):** quoted war-risk premiums for Saudi-linked tankers calling at Yanbu have risen to **around 3% of a vessel's value**, from less than 1% in early July, before London's marine market designated the water high-risk; for ports south of Yanbu including Jizan, premiums around 1% in early July can now run higher. Saudi Arabia is building volumes back through the East-West line but **loadings at Yanbu have yet to resume**, per industry sources, satellite images and shipping data ([Reuters via MarineLink Sep 24](https://www.marinelink.com/news/oil-tanker-insurance-triples-amidst-543249)). **Agent arithmetic and inference (medium confidence):** 3% of a ~$120m VLCC is ~$3.6m a voyage, roughly **$1.80/bbl** on a full cargo — against the **$25–30/bbl** it costs to move the same barrel out of Hormuz under record freight. On price alone Yanbu should dominate by more than an order of magnitude. That it is empty means the binding constraints are pipeline throughput and owner willingness, not premium — and neither is fixed by a further insurance move.

- **The route's air defence is European and it is firing (Sep 24, new):** an air-defence system operated by **Greek military personnel** in Saudi Arabia intercepted a ballistic missile and a drone in the wider Yanbu region on Thursday, the Greek Patriot launching two missiles, a Greek security source told Reuters; the Saudi-led coalition separately said six Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at Taif and Yanbu were destroyed ([Al Jazeera/Reuters/AP Sep 24](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/24/saudi-led-coalition-says-shot-down-6-ballistic-missiles-launched-by-houthis)). ISW assesses that international air-defence missions protecting Saudi oil infrastructure now pose a dilemma for the Houthis by expanding international action against the group ([ISW Sep 25](https://understandingwar.org/research/middle-east/iran-update-september-25-2026)). **Read:** the pattern established Sep 22 completes — UK refuelling, French deployment, Greek interceptors. ## On the Ground in Iran
*Last materially changed: 2026-09-24 (AP reports from the Gürbulak crossing: 7-km queues and dwell times up from a maximum of three days to more than five — the first hard measurement of the overland ceiling, and the trigger condition carried on Q2 since Sep 20)*

**Sep 24 UPDATE — the land border, finally measured:**

- **Gürbulak/Bazargan dwell times up roughly two-thirds (AP, Sep 24, new):** AP reporters at Turkey's largest crossing into Iran describe hundreds of trucks idling and lines stretching for miles. A driver with 15 years on the route, Muhammed Rıza Shiri, 55, waited **four days** to carry a load of rubber into Iran, at the end of a **7-kilometre** queue: "We used to wait, maximum, for three days here. Because of the war, the waiting times exceed five days." Pezeshkian has pressed to accelerate overland trade as the blockade cuts off the majority of imports ([AP Sep 24](https://apnews.com/article/iran-turkey-border-trade-trucks-delays-war-11f510a93bdf56754f994faff99b3e02)).
- **Agent arithmetic and inference (medium-high confidence):** border dwell has gone from ≤3 days to >5, i.e. **+67% or more**. On a notional 10-day round trip of which three days were waiting, a move to five-plus days lengthens the cycle to 12+ days — a **17–20% reduction in trips per truck per year** for an essentially fixed regional fleet. Because the constraint is queue time rather than road capacity, extra trucks do not relieve it; only customs throughput or new gates do. This is the quantitative form of Fortune's Sep 20 "red tape and gridlock" finding and **fires the Q2 trigger condition carried since Sep 20**.
- **Read:** both of Iran's substitution channels are now demonstrably capacity-capped rather than scaling — the Caspian at a fixed 0.9 kt per call, the land border at a fixed number of customs slots per day. That is the analytical core: further tightening of the naval blockade has low marginal economic value, while Turkish customs throughput and the two crossings under negotiation are now the highest-leverage variables in Iran's import position.

**Sep 23 UPDATE (carried) — the inversion holds, the base keeps shrinking:**

- **Port-call split, week to Sep 18 (PortWatch):** Caspian **47 calls** (Amirabad 22, Anzali 15, Nowshahr 10) against **17** across all eleven monitored Gulf ports. Same week 2025: Caspian 15, Gulf 44. The inversion is now four weeks old and deepening on the Gulf side — Gulf calls fell from 23 to 17 week-on-week, with Bandar Shahid Rajaee down from 9 calls to 2 and zero tanker calls at every Iranian Gulf port in the set.
- **Tonnage (agent arithmetic on PortWatch):** Caspian imports **43 kt on 47 calls = 0.91 kt/call**; Gulf imports **133 kt on 17 calls = 7.8 kt/call**. Total visible seaborne imports **176 kt, −24% week-on-week** (from 231 kt) and now roughly half the 2025 level. Caspian share of visible import tonnage **24%**, from 20%.

**Sep 20 UPDATE (carried) — the northern pivot, measured:**

- **Iran's port-call geography inverted (PortWatch, week to Sep 11):** Caspian 51 calls against 23 in the Gulf; same week 2025 Caspian 22 / Gulf 46. Caspian calls +132% YoY; Gulf calls −50%.
- **But the tonnage is small:** Caspian imports 46 kt on 51 calls; Gulf 185 kt on 23 calls. Caspian share of visible seaborne imports rose from ~4% to ~20%; the Caspian gain of 30 kt offset **19%** of the Gulf's 156 kt loss.
- **Read:** the substitution is real, high-frequency and roughly a ninth as efficient per movement — sufficient to sustain essential goods and structurally incapable of replacing export earnings. The land-border and Caspian-feeder ledger (Astara, Sarakhs, Bazargan) now determines Iran's import capacity, which caps the marginal economic value of tightening the naval blockade further.
