# Iran War — Standing Sections
*Slow-moving sections of the daily briefing live here, in full. The daily briefing carries ONE bullet + link per section, and reprints a section in full only on the day it materially changes. Each header carries a "last materially changed" date so readers can see staleness at a glance.*

**Update protocol (daily agent):** For each section below, ask one question each run: *did anything material change in the last 24 hours?* If no — touch nothing here, and the briefing bullet reads "unchanged since [date]". If yes — update the section here, bump its date, and summarize the delta in the briefing's "What changed" list. Never copy a full section back into the briefing.

## Iranian State Disintegration Tracker — Iran's Endurance Ceiling
*Last materially changed: 2026-09-21 (rial 2,310,100/USD, third consecutive weaker session; gold-coin premium fades back to ~1.0% as Saturday's hard-asset outperformance reverses; Tether discount widens to 1.2%; civil ADS-B −23% week-on-week)*

**Economic (confirmed, carried):** IMF −5.4% GDP projection 2026; war damage ~$270B (~90% pre-war GDP); inflation 88.6%+ (rural >100%); $27B capital flight 2025; autos −47%, petrochemicals −17%; approved foreign investment −80%; 20M+ bbl stranded afloat; China imports halved to 654K bpd (June); TGL revoked; dark-fleet channel only. Unemployment 9.1% (from 7.3%), 450,000 jobs lost.

**Sep 21 UPDATE — the hard-asset blip did not persist:**

- **Rial 2,310,100 IRR/USD (tgju, Sep 21):** 0.3% weaker than Sep 20 and 1% weaker than Sep 18; a third consecutive down session and ~15.5% cumulative depreciation against the ~2.0M/USD of late August. NCRI separately reported the rate crossing 232,000 toman as retirees marched and parliament widened security controls ([NCRI Sep 20](https://www.ncr-iran.org/en/news/economy/rial-slides-to-232000-as-retirees-march-and-parliament-widens-irans-security-controls)) — opposition-sourced framing, but the rate is consistent with tgju.

- **Gold-coin premium fades to ~1.0% (agent arithmetic on tgju, Sep 21):** Emami coin 2,365M IRR = ~$1,024 at the open-market rate against ~$1,014 intrinsic (7.32g fine; world-gold assumption carried from Sep 19). The coin fell 0.4% in rial while the currency depreciated 0.3% — Saturday's 1.7% premium and first hard-asset outperformance have partially reversed within one session. 18k gram 238.4M IRR. **Read:** the managed-bleed characterisation holds; there is still no hard-asset scramble, and the one-day move on Sep 20 was noise, not a trend.

- **Tether discount widens to 1.2% (tgju, Sep 21):** 2,282,240 IRR versus the 2,310,100 cash dollar, from 0.7% on Sep 20 and 0.2% on Sep 19. A widening discount on the crypto rail is the opposite of a flight signal. Context: US Treasury has sanctioned the Iranian exchange BitBank, which it says routed hundreds of millions of dollars to the IRGC and processed Hormuz passage payments ([JPost/Reuters Sep 18](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/iran-news/2026-09-18/live-updates-909020)).

- **Connectivity and airspace (IODA, OpenSky Sep 21):** 11,057 reachable /24s against a prior six-day mean of 11,184 (−1.1%), with the 24-hour minimum equal to the latest reading — no intraday trough, unlike Sep 20's 10,660. BGP prefixes 41,697, flat. Iranian civil ADS-B **71 aircraft**, down from 83 on Sep 20 and 92 a week ago (−23% w/w). **Inference (low-medium confidence):** two consecutive declines in civil aviation activity against flat connectivity is more consistent with airspace caution ahead of an expected US package — Iran's military command said Sunday it had been informed strikes are being prepared — than with infrastructure damage. Worth watching; not yet a signal.

- **Currency transmission into industry (Sep 21):** ILNA reports coal mines cannot obtain imported safety and gas-monitoring equipment as the exchange rate rises; "the more expensive foreign currency becomes, the less financial capacity employers have to provide safety" ([Iran International Sep 21](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202609218799)). A concrete channel from depreciation to industrial capacity loss and worker risk.

**Sep 19–20 UPDATE (carried) — the hard-asset panic that should be present is not:**

- **Tehran gold-coin premium ≈ 0% (Sep 19), 1.7% (Sep 20) — agent arithmetic on tgju.** Historically Iranian coin premiums ("hobab") have run 20–40% in acute stress. **Inference (medium confidence):** households liquidating gold to fund consumption are absorbing capital-flight demand; the full adjustment is running through the exchange rate rather than through a hard-asset scramble. This argues for a managed, grinding deterioration rather than an imminent monetary break.

- **Tether sanctions exposure (Sep 18):** US Treasury has publicly tied a Tehran crypto exchange to the handling of part of Iran's $2M Hormuz passage fees ([eGamers/Treasury Sep 18](https://egamers.io/treasury-tehran-crypto-exchange-handled-part-of-irans-2-million-hormuz-passage-fees)) — sanctions exposure is rising on exactly the rail that a panic would use.

- **State functioning through the Mahsa Amini anniversary (Sep 19):** The regime did not throttle the network despite reported shop strikes in western Iran ([Iran International Sep 16](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202609167981)). Either confidence in control or an unwillingness to bear the economic cost of a shutdown; the second is more consistent with the currency data.

- **Iran-Oman route agreement: signing did not occur Sep 14 (correction adopted Sep 17, carried):** Framework announced but unsigned. Pragmatist track weakened but structurally present.

- **Dual-track bifurcation status (carried from Sep 18):** Hardliner track reinforced by continuing IRGC maritime enforcement. Rezaei's conditions are now transmitted rather than merely declared — see Q9 in the questions ledger.

**Mass mobilisation campaign (Sep 18, carried):** IRGC commander Hassan Hassanzadeh said more than 600,000 people had registered for the "Janfaday-e Iran" limited-training campaign with over 1 million expected; IRNA estimated more than 300,000 took to the streets on Sep 18 ([CBS Sep 18](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/iran-war-trump-us-strait-of-hormuz-oil-houthis)). State-choreographed; a mobilisation-capacity signal, not a capability one.

**IAEA Sep 9 resolution and UNSC referral (confirmed, carried):** Board resolution adopted Sep 9; UNSC referral confirmed. Russia/China veto blocks enforcement.

**Military reconstitution:** Iran **claims** >75% of pre-war missile/drone capability retained (CBS, Aug 12 — **Iranian claim**). International reporting indicates Iran has resumed assembling ballistic missiles in underground facilities from pre-war components, at a reduced scale ([Alma Sep 16](https://israel-alma.org/the-iranian-arena-monthly-review-august-september-2026)). Chinese air defense equipment delivered (ISW Sep 15).

**Fleet attrition (carried):** Eight Iranian tankers struck/sunk Sep 5–8. Dark-fleet channel only for oil exports.

**Trajectory:** Tipping point Q1–Q2 2027 (operative baseline), unchanged. The currency and hard-asset data argue the near-term path is grinding erosion rather than discontinuity; nothing in today's data pulls the tipping point forward.

## Red Sea / Bab el-Mandeb — Baseline
*Last materially changed: 2026-09-21 (Houthis strike Riyadh with a ballistic missile and target Yanbu oil infrastructure for the first time since the East-West pipeline outage; oil gives back the entire move — the Saudi-front headline premium is exhausted)*

**Sep 21 UPDATE — the first strike at Riyadh, and the market shrugged:**

- **Riyadh targeted with a ballistic missile; Yanbu, Taif, Baysh and Farasan also attacked (Sep 19, new):** Saudi Arabia confirmed the Riyadh attempt was intercepted — the first targeting of the capital since the escalation opened a new front in the Iran war. Residents heard an explosion and some saw smoke near the airport; no casualties or damage reported. The coalition said attempts on Yanbu — where the East-West pipeline terminates — and three other locations were thwarted. The Houthis claimed without evidence that they hit "sensitive sites" in Riyadh and Aramco facilities at Yanbu causing "massive fires"; Aramco did not respond to a request for comment ([AP Sep 19](https://apnews.com/article/yemen-saudi-arabia-b1286cad816dd3e553f50f6dd5205972); [NPR Sep 19](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/19/nx-s1-5974980/saudi-arabia-houthis-attempted-attack-capital)). Explosions were also reported at Jizan and Abha ([Newsquawk Sep 21](https://www.newsquawk.com/headlines/newsquawk-daily-asia-pac-opening-news---21st-september-2026)).

- **Price response: none that held.** Crude opened firmer in Asia on the Yanbu/Riyadh reports (WTI +0.7%) and then retreated through the session with no fresh news behind the reversal, a fourth consecutive down day ([Newsquawk Sep 21](https://newsquawk.com/blog/5769-eu-market-open-trump-threatens-blowing-up-all-of-iran-whilst-houthis-target-a-saudi-oil-facility-crude-prices-initially-firmer-but-have-since-fallen)). **Read (inference, medium-high confidence):** Yanbu loadings are already at 37 kt/week, ~5% of the 2025 week (PortWatch) and the pipeline has been offline since Sep 11, so a Houthi strike there removes no incremental barrel. The Saudi-front headline premium is exhausted; tradeable Saudi supply risk now sits at Ras Tanura and the Gulf shuttle system, not the Red Sea coast.

- **Saudi missile-defence depletion (Sep 16, new to this section):** Regional officials say Riyadh has asked France, Britain, Pakistan and Egypt to deploy air-defence support as its interceptor stocks run low ([LA Times Sep 16](https://www.latimes.com/world-nation/story/2026-09-16/saudi-arabia-calls-attempted-attack-on-mecca-red-line-as-iran-backed-houthis-deny-they-did-it)). Turkey's foreign minister says Ankara is ready to meet Saudi military needs ([Turkish Minute Sep 20](https://www.turkishminute.com?p=186787)). **Implication:** the constraint on the Saudi front is now defensive magazine depth, not political will — the same constraint the US faces.

**Carried — the corridor is impaired, not closed:**

- **PortWatch, week to Sep 13 (revised):** Bab el-Mandeb **27.3 transits/day, 8.1 tankers, 438k DWT/day**; tanker DWT −31% vs 2025 and **+29% week-on-week**. Suez 37.9 transits/day, 15.9 tankers, 687k DWT/day, −18% vs 2025. For contrast, Hormuz tanker DWT is −99% vs 2025.

- **CORRECTION (Sep 19, standing):** The line carried in this section from Sep 14 — that Bab el-Mandeb is at "structural near-zero for commercial shipping" — was wrong and is withdrawn. Houthi territorial control of the Yemeni Red Sea coast, Mocha and Perim/Mayyun Island is confirmed and unchanged; control of the shoreline has not translated into closure of the waterway.

- **Lloyd's List Intelligence, Red Sea Brief (Sep 18):** Suez transits totalled **1,232 in August, +28% YoY**, tonnage +43% MoM — the busiest month since December 2023. Bab el-Mandeb recorded **1,165 voyages, +6% YoY but −49% MoM**. **44 ultra-large containerships have transited Bab el-Mandeb this year, 39 of them since July**, versus one in all of 2025. ## Gulf States Adaptation
*Last materially changed: 2026-09-21 (the Hormuz shuttle system documented: STS off Sohar/Fujairah, 24–40h per transfer, ~15% of the global VLCC fleet tied up; Saudi loadings "climbing back toward several million bpd" by a brutally inefficient method)*

**Sep 21 UPDATE — the mechanism behind the dark flow, quantified:**

- **Hormuz did not close cleanly; it became a shuttle system (Sep 19, new):** Shuttle tankers enter the Gulf, load, run the strait, and transfer cargo ship-to-ship off Sohar (Oman) or Fujairah to long-haul VLCCs that never enter Hormuz. Each STS transfer takes **24 to 40 hours**; shuttles then cycle back through the strait while long-haul VLCCs queue in the Gulf of Oman. Brokers estimate **~15% of the global VLCC fleet is tied up** to some degree in this system, and soaring freight is already making some distant crude trades uneconomic, with barely any VLCCs left for hire in parts of the market ([EnergyNewsBeat citing Bloomberg, Sep 19](https://energynewsbeat.co/crude-oil/the-global-oil-market-is-short-vlcc-tankers-and-needs-them-to-rebalance)). Saudi Arabia intensified the workaround after the East-West pipeline outage; loadings "have climbed back toward several million barrels a day, but the method is brutally inefficient." **Read:** this is the physical explanation for the gap between AIS-visible tonnage (13k DWT/day) and Cooper's cumulative barrel tally — and it converts a chokepoint problem into a global fleet-capacity problem, which is why the tonne-mile shock persists even if Hormuz risk eases.

- **Corroborating AIS work (Sep 20, new):** Independent tracking identified 11 VLCCs sailing directly from Fujairah to Japan and 33 Middle East–Japan tankers in total including STS off Malacca, with at least one Fujairah departure loaded from an offshore SBM ([Watanabe Lab/note.com Sep 20](https://note.com/hwtnv/n/nc88b869c2e0c?hl=en)) — single-source OSINT, consistent with the shuttle/STS picture.
