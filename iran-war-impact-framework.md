The IRGC's "scale and nature" escalation threat moved from warning to execution: Iran fired seven ballistic missiles at Kuwait and Bahrain simultaneously — the largest single ballistic salvo against US-allied Gulf states since the war began — six intercepted by CENTCOM, the seventh failing to reach its target. The attack hit two separate US-allied Gulf states simultaneously — Kuwait (second strike in four days) and Bahrain (host to NSA Bahrain, US Fifth Fleet HQ) — more than tripling the June 1 salvo count. Iran framed the attack as retaliation for CENTCOM's Goruk and Qeshm strikes. This establishes saturation ballistic missile attacks on multiple Gulf capitals simultaneously as normalized within IRGC operating doctrine. The prior escalation ladder was: drone/missile vs. military targets (March–April) → civilian airport (June 3) → seven-missile dual-capital ballistic salvo (June 6).

Third Qeshm strike of the conflict; fourth confirmed US self-defense strike since the April ceasefire. The attack-retaliation-counter-retaliation cycle now runs on a daily operational cadence with no diplomatic circuit-breaker functioning.
- **IRAN THREATENS "COMPLETE HORMUZ CLOSURE" (June 6, Gulf News / arabtimesonline)**: Iran warned that continued US strikes would trigger a "complete closure" of the Strait of Hormuz. This is qualitatively different from all prior warnings: it explicitly frames complete closure as an imminent operational response to US self-defense strikes rather than a held-in-reserve strategic ultimatum. Brent at ~$94.70 has not priced a complete closure scenario — the IEA has already characterized the existing partial blockade as the "largest supply disruption in the history of the global oil market"; a formal complete closure goes beyond that baseline.
- **HEZBOLLAH REJECTS LEBANON-ISRAEL CONDITIONAL CEASEFIRE (June 4–5, NPR / Al Jazeera / Euronews)**: Israel and Lebanon reached a conditional ceasefire framework in Washington talks (June 2–3), but Hezbollah publicly rejected it, demanding instead a comprehensive truce and full Israeli withdrawal from Lebanon. This creates a structural paradox at the heart of Iran's diplomatic posture: Iran requires a Lebanon ceasefire as its number-one precondition for resuming formal negotiations, but Iran's own proxy Hezbollah is blocking the available ceasefire from taking effect. Iran cannot credibly claim to want a Lebanon ceasefire while operationally preventing one. More than 20 people were killed in Israeli strikes in Lebanon on June 6 as fighting continues despite the government-level ceasefire agreement.
- **LEBANESE PRESIDENT AOUN: IRAN "USING LEBANON AS A BARGAINING CHIP" (June 6, CNN)**: Lebanese President Joseph Aoun publicly told CNN that Iran "must stop interfering in Lebanon" and that Tehran is "using Lebanon as a bargaining chip in their negotiation with the United States." This is the most significant Lebanese sovereign statement separating Lebanese governmental interests from Iranian strategic objectives of the entire conflict. It publicly exposes the instrumentalization that Iran has been executing through Hezbollah's ceasefire veto — and does so from Lebanon's head of state rather than a diaspora or opposition source. Hezbollah rejected this framing via Al Manar.
- **VIX CLOSES AT 21.51 (+40%) — LARGEST SINGLE-SESSION REPRICING OF CONFLICT (June 6)**: VIX closed at 21.51 (+6.11 points, +39.68%), rising from the 15.32 conflict low set June 4. The information war for the Lebanese audience is now a three-way contest between Aoun's sovereignty framing, Hezbollah's resistance framing, and Iran's "Lebanon solidarity" framing, with each carrying implications for whether Iran's Lebanon precondition can be used to indefinitely block formal negotiations.
