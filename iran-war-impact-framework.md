**Key developments:**
- **IAEA DG GROSSI: NUCLEAR INSPECTIONS "GOING TO HAPPEN" — TIMING "NOT ESSENTIAL" (NPR / Euronews / CBS News, June 24):** IAEA Director General Rafael Grossi confirmed that nuclear site inspections in Iran "are going to happen" under the MOU, directly countering FM Baghaei's June 23 denial. Grossi's precise framing — "whether this happens the day after tomorrow or in one week or in 10 days, it's important, but not essential" — introduces a flexible timeline that technically validates Baghaei's "not yet" without conceding his "war-damaged sites" limitation. This is the first IAEA principal-level public statement since the Versailles signing; it confirms the inspections track is operationally live while deliberately not specifying which sites will be accessible. The nuclear working group at Burgenstock remains the venue for converting Grossi's commitment into site-specific access protocols.
- **BRENT ~$75.47/BBL; VIX 19.49 — MOST EXTREME IMPLEMENTATION-PHASE DIVERGENCE (Trading Economics / TradingView, June 24):** Brent fell from $77.42 (June 23) to approximately $75.47–76.40 — its lowest level since before the war began; from the April 30 intraday peak of $126.41, approximately 90–92% of the war premium has now unwound. Simultaneously, VIX rose to 19.49, sharply above the 13–15 range estimated for June 23 and the highest since the June 21 IRGC closure declaration. The Brent-VIX divergence is the primary market signal of the day: oil futures price high deal-execution confidence while equity-options markets price elevated tail risk from Lebanon implementation uncertainty and IAEA access ambiguity. Gold fell to $4,124.22/oz (−1.60%); DXY strengthened above 100 — FOMC hawkish dot continues to dominate both.
- **TWO MAJOR IRAN-ALIGNED IRAQI MILITIAS SIGNAL STATE INTEGRATION — PROXY NETWORK FRACTURE (The National, June 20; confirmed June 24):** Asaib Ahl al-Haq (led by Qais al-Khazali, longtime Tehran ally) and Saraya al-Salam (led by Moqtada al-Sadr) publicly announced willingness to integrate into the Iraqi state security apparatus — the most significant Iranian proxy network fracture of the conflict. Kataib Hezbollah, the most IRGC-loyal of the three Iraqi groups, refused integration and remains aligned with IRGC command. The split converts the Iraqi proxy bloc from a unified Iran-directed force into a contested question within Iraqi domestic politics, structurally reducing the organized drone-attack threat to Saudi Eastern Province and Bahrain — Gulf states' primary security exposure since the June 3 Kuwait airport strike.
- **QATARENERGY EXTENDS LNG FORCE MAJEURE TO MID-AUGUST (gasworld / Bloomberg, June 2026):** QatarEnergy extended its LNG force majeure from mid-June to mid-August 2026, covering 17 cargoes (~2.2 billion cubic metres) including deliveries to Adriatic LNG in Italy. Two of 14 LNG trains and one of two GTL facilities are damaged; infrastructure repairs estimated at 3–5 years. Structurally significant alongside the August 16–21 dual-expiry cliff: if Phase 2 nuclear talks reach mid-August without a framework, Qatar's force majeure, PGSA toll suspension, and oil TGL all expire in the same five-day window — compounding Iran's structural incentive to stretch Phase 2 rather than close early. The four QatarEnergy tankers that entered Hormuz on June 22–23 are position-management moves, not cargo delivery; they do not conflict with the force majeure extension.
- **PEZESHKIAN IN PAKISTAN — MISSILE STATEMENT AND BACK-CHANNEL IAEA SIGNAL (Al Jazeera, June 23–24):** Iranian President Pezeshkian's Pakistan visit (first presidential foreign travel since Versailles) produced a public missile statement — "The discussion over our missiles does not exist in the MoU, and it never will" — aimed at domestic hardliner audiences. The visit's operational function is almost certainly to deliver Iran's actual IAEA position to Pakistan for relay to Washington, maintaining the diplomatic fiction that Iran has "not formally agreed" to full inspectors while operationally facilitating their arrival at non-war-damaged enrichment sites. Consistent with all six prior principal-level contradictions resolving at the working-group level.
- **IMO STRANDED SEAFARERS EVACUATION PLAN — TWO TEMPORARY MARITIME CORRIDORS (CNBC, June 23):** The IMO launched an evacuation plan for 11,000+ stranded seafarers via two temporary maritime corridors backed by both Iran and the US — the first formal bilateral coordination mechanism for vessel movements. On June 24, approximately 23 ships transited Hormuz, up from 12 AIS-visible on June 21, and roughly 20% of the pre-war 110+/day baseline.
