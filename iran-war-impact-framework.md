**Day 259 / Week 37.** The Houthis have turned onto Saudi Arabia's Hormuz bypass — the East–West pipeline and Yanbu — and the Bab el-Mandeb tanker leg has given back its entire October recovery. Brent's only up-day of the week came from the Red Sea, not the Gulf.

### The calls — where the read moved

**Q1 · Gulf flows — the dark-flow gap is now measurable, and it is roughly fiftyfold.**
The week to Nov 6 clears yesterday's deferred test. Inside-Gulf loadings — Ras Tanura 372 kt, Ras Laffan 425, Jebel Ali 274 — total **1,071 kt against 629 the prior week (+70%)**, or 21% of 2025's 5,110 kt. Over the same days Hormuz AIS tanker tonnage fell **78% to 3k DWT/day** (PortWatch). **Agent arithmetic: 1,071 ÷ 7 = 153 kt/day of cargo leaving terminals whose only sea exit is the strait; carried cargo cannot exceed deadweight, so ≥150k DWT/day must transit against 3k visible — about 2%.** The average AIS-visible "tanker" at Hormuz is now **3,000 DWT against 47,400 in 2025** — coastal craft, not carriers. Fujairah loaded 997 kt on 40 calls versus 2025's 893 kt on 68: **24.9 kt per call against 13.1**, the STS-aggregation signature. Confidence high. Fujairah calls below ~30 would break it.

**Q7 · Red Sea — October's tanker normalisation has fully reversed, and the target set has moved to the bypass itself.**
Bab el-Mandeb tanker DWT: 554k/day in the week to Oct 18 (−5% vs 2025) → **343k in the week to Nov 8, −61% vs 2025 and −38% in three weeks** (PortWatch). The Oct 24 normalisation read is withdrawn. Suez tanker tonnage is flat at 780k, +1% vs 2025, so the **Suez-minus-Bab gap widened from 287k to 437k DWT/day**. *Agent inference (medium): a growing share of Suez tanker traffic is Red Sea-internal — Yanbu, SUMED, Jeddah — not through-traffic from the Gulf of Aden.* That is precisely the flow the Houthis claimed on Nov 12: an East–West pipeline pumping station and Yanbu oil facilities, on a route Riyadh uses for ~4 mb/d ([Al Jazeera, Nov 12](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/12/yemens-houthis-renew-strikes-on-saudi-arabia-as-unsc-extends-sanctions)). Confidence medium-high. Saudi confirmation of pipeline downtime moves it again.

**Q6 · Israel — the General Staff has written itself out of the initiator role.**
Channel 13, via ToI and a ten-outlet comparison, reports the IDF told Netanyahu and Eisenkot in Thursday's two-hour briefing that **there is no urgent need for an Israeli strike**, while supporting Israeli participation in any American operation Trump orders; Zamir says that if Israel is compelled to act, the objective would be **toppling the regime** ([ToI, Nov 12](https://www.timesofisrael.com/in-netanyahu-eisenkot-briefing-idf-reportedly-says-no-urgent-need-to-strike-iran); [NewsCord, Nov 13](https://newscord.org/article/israeli-army-tells-netanyahu-and-eisenkot-it-sees-no-urgent-need-to-strike-iran--Story_20261113_75d3f56c)). *Agent inference (medium-high): regime change is not achievable with a short package, which is why the staff that owns that objective will not go first. Israel is now a follower whose entry is conditioned on a US decision — this supersedes yesterday's authority-vacuum framing with a military reason rather than a legal one.* Home Front logged one rockets/missiles alert today, the first hostile event since Nov 8 (Home Front alerts). A published Nov 15 cabinet authorisation reverses it.
