**Information Warfare:** The central narrative battle today is whether a deal is being negotiated and on whose terms Hormuz reopens. Trump is simultaneously claiming talks are ongoing "at Iran's request" (Aug 3) and signaling he is prepared to "low-key" them in favor of economic pressure (CNBC, Aug 10) — a deliberate ambiguity designed to maintain domestic political flexibility while keeping Iran uncertain about the military timeline. Tehran is countering by publicly denying direct talks exist and insisting it deals only through Oman, while issuing new Hormuz compensation demands that Trump has publicly rejected as a "toll racket." The CNBC Aug 10 headline — "Trump signals shift to economic pressure as Iran hardens stance over Strait of Hormuz" — captures the day's dominant frame: neither side is preparing to close a deal today. Israel's reported "Operation Blue-and-White" unilateral strike preparation (CBN NewsWatch, Aug 6; Mirror Now, Aug 9) functions as an Israeli information operation pressuring Washington not to accept any deal leaving Iran's nuclear and missile capabilities intact — and pressuring Iran with the specter of strikes without US restraint. The Houthi Mokha/Jazan strikes serve Iranian information warfare by demonstrating that the proxy network remains operationally active and costly for Gulf states even without direct Iranian resupply. Iran is currently winning the "are talks happening" tactical battle and the "Hormuz sovereignty" narrative in its domestic audience; the US is winning the economic-pressure and free-navigation frame with Gulf partners. Neither side is winning the "deal is imminent" argument because no deal is structurally within reach today.
