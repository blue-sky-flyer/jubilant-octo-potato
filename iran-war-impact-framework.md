## Daily Briefing - 2026-10-18

**Day 233 / Week 34.** The Hormuz tracker split that I flagged as unresolved yesterday is resolved today by CENTCOM's own number, and it resolves in favour of the low figure. The more consequential finding is underneath it: the Saudi Red Sea bypass is not working, which means the strait is carrying *more* of the Gulf's export burden than the "40% now bypasses Hormuz" framing implies.

### The calls — where the read moved

**Q1 · Flows — the bypass has failed, and that is why Hormuz dependence is rising while loadings fall.**
Misbar, working MarineTraffic risk-and-compliance records on Bahri hulls, reports that pumping through Saudi Arabia's East–West pipeline **halted days after the Sep 10 attack**, that Yanbu loadings were suspended, and that Saudi crude shifted to Gulf ports with AIS transmission gaps and probable STS ([Misbar, Oct 16](https://www.misbar.com/en/editorial/2026/10/16/after-east%E2%80%93west-pipeline-attack-misbar-tracks-saudi-tankers-moving-oil-covertly-through-hormuz)). That gives the Yanbu print a cause: 1,442 kt/week in 2025 → 22 kt, roughly **1.5 mb/d of Red Sea outlet offline**, while Ras Tanura loadings nearly doubled week-on-week (163 → 313 kt) as barrels rerouted east (PortWatch). The EPRINC/Kpler "40% bypass" share is therefore stale. Separately, Cooper's claim of ~100m bbl and ~400 ships in a week = **14.3 mb/d**, which matches Kpler's **14 mb/d regional** total including pipelines ([CNBC, Oct 14](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/14/container-ship-sinks-strait-hormuz-as-iran-war-attacks.html)), not its 8.5 mb/d strait figure — yesterday's definitional inference is confirmed. Confidence high. Changes on a dated Saudi confirmation that East–West has restarted.

**Q3 · Capital flows — my own falsification test tripped, at a magnitude below the instrument's noise.**
Yesterday I wrote that a Tether premium appearing *while* the rial strengthens would break the supply-side read. Today the rial firms 1% to 2,613,650 and Tether prints 2,614,980 — a **+0.1% premium**, the first in four weeks (tgju). Two reasons not to break the read: the swing is 0.3pp, inside the board's own noise; and the same day's 18k gram at 259.2M implies **~$4,113/oz fine** against a world ounce of $4,146 ([Kitco via Vietnam.vn, Oct 16](https://www.vietnam.vn/en/gia-vang-sang-nay-16-10-2026-tiep-tuc-di-ngang)) — still a 0.8% **discount**, narrowed from 1.9%. Emami in dollars is flat at ~$1,012. Both hard-asset bases are converging to par as the rate firms, which is the supply-side prediction, not flight demand's. Confidence cut from high to **medium-high**. Changes on Tether above +1% for two consecutive weeks.

**Q8 · Mecca Pact — it has crossed from declaration to deployment, and it is widening past its three signatories.**
Pakistan's military says its forces are in the kingdom in "multiple capabilities and domains" ([JPost/Media Line, Oct 12](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-911413)); the Oct 5 Riyadh emergency meeting committed signatories to "rapidly deploy agreed forces and capabilities", with numbers, missions and timetables unstated ([New Arab, Oct 12](https://www.newarab.com/analysis/can-turkey-and-pakistan-defend-saudi-arabia-houthis)); Pakistan and Türkiye have since agreed to deploy ([National Herald India, Oct 15](https://www.nationalheraldindia.com/international/pakistan-trkiye-agree-to-deploy-forces-in-saudi-arabia-under-trilateral-pact)). Egypt — not a signatory — asked parliament on Oct 17 for emergency authorisation to intervene in Saudi defence, with the House convening Oct 18 ([Business Insider Africa, Oct 18](https://africa.businessinsider.com/local/markets/a-powerful-african-military-considers-joining-pakistan-and-turkey-in-defending-saudi/y5e0nvc)). **Inference (medium): this is air- and missile-defence backfill against a 20-attack salvo rate, not an expeditionary force — none of the four has confirmed combat troops.** Confidence medium-high. Changes on a signatory striking targets inside Yemen.

## Model cross-check

- **Resolved — watch item (1).** PortWatch did not advance; a third identical file. The four-terminal aggregate stays a single observation. I am now treating the PortWatch lag as structurally longer than the advertised 4–5 days and will not plan analysis around a weekly cadence.
- **Resolved — watch item (2), partially against me.** The Tether discount did flip, to +0.1%, while the rial appreciated. By the letter of yesterday's test that breaks the supply-side read; by magnitude it does not, and the gold basis on the same board still shows a discount. I have downgraded confidence rather than claim the test was badly specified. If next week prints a second premium with a firmer rial, the read flips to "returning dollar demand" and Q3 is rewritten.
- **Resolved — the Hormuz tracker split.** CENTCOM's ~14.3 mb/d implied figure sits on top of Kpler's 14 mb/d *regional* number, which is the strongest available evidence that the competing series are measuring different objects. **Caveat, material: the "400 ships / 100 million barrels" figures come from a non-primary summary of Cooper's Oct 16 video ([YouTube, Oct 16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SJGzWZfGGlw)); the primary carriers of the same statement ([Iran International, Oct 16](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202610162840); [Times of Israel, Oct 17](https://www.timesofisrael.com/centcom-chief-reports-irans-proxies-holding-back-salaries-to-send-money-to-irgc)) carry the $25bn and $350m figures but not the throughput line. Single-sourced; used for reconciliation arithmetic only, not adopted into any series.**
- **New, logged not adopted — Iran's exports are not strictly zero.** Reuters, via four maritime-security sources, reports STS transfers off Oman concealing at least **eight LPG tankers and one jet-fuel tanker** exiting the Gulf of Oman; ISW calls the volume "very limited and relatively inconsequential" ([ISW/CTP, Oct 16](https://www.criticalthreats.org/analysis/iran-update-october-16-2026)). This is product, not crude, and does not contradict Vortexa's crude zero — but "no exports and no imports by sea", as CENTCOM put it, is now too strong.
- **Unresolved — the attack-efficacy contradiction.** A CENTCOM spokesman says the "vast majority" of Iranian attempts on Hormuz fail ([Fox, Oct 17](https://www.foxnews.com/world/vast-majority-irans-attempted-attacks-strait-hormuz-unsuccessful-centcom-spokesman-says)), while UKMTO-derived counts put October at a record and three hulls have now sunk. Both can be true if the denominator is launches; neither side publishes it.
- **Watch for contradiction next run:** (1) whether Egypt's parliament actually authorises, and whether any signatory names a mission; (2) whether the Tether premium persists or reverts; (3) whether any dated source confirms East–West pipeline repair — that is the single number that would most change the Gulf export arithmetic.

### Qwen
I disagree with Claude’s confidence in the “bypass has failed” read. PortWatch shows Fujairah exports rose to 732 kt/week (vs. 667 kt prior week), and Yanbu’s 22 kt is negligible — but **Bab el-Mandeb transits remain stable at 8.6 tankers/day (436k DWT)**, unchanged from prior week, and **Suez tankers rose to 17.0/day (643k DWT)**, up from 15.6. This contradicts the claim that the Red Sea bypass is “not working.” The 1.5 mb/d drop in Yanbu is offset by increased flows via Suez and Bab el-Mandeb — the bypass is functioning, just not at pre-war scale. Hormuz’s 0.6 tankers/day is low, but the regional system is adapting. Claude misreads the data: the burden isn’t shifting *to* Hormuz — it’s being *distributed* across multiple routes.
