## Daily Briefing — 2026-06-12

**Overall situation:** Day 105 / Week 15. Trump announced from the White House that "we just made a great settlement of the war with Iran," saying documents should be finalized within days and a signing could come as early as this weekend. Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesman Baqaei immediately contradicted him: Tehran has "not reached a final conclusion on the agreement" and complications arose when "the US side made new demands and changed its positions." Iran's semi-official Mehr News published what it says is a draft 14-point MOU, and Iran state TV confirmed the existence of a text — so a document exists, but the gap on US demands persists. Against that backdrop, the US military shot down two Iranian attack drones near the Strait of Hormuz after Iranian forces fired on a transiting vessel, confirming that the June 11 shoot-on-sight declaration has been at least partially executed. Brent fell to $87.43, entering the deal-proximity range ($85–90) for the first time since the complete closure was declared. The key binary is whether Trump and Khamenei can both formally authorize the MOU before either a second Iranian enforcement action or an Israeli unilateral strike on a nuclear site collapses the window.

**Key developments:**
- **TRUMP "GREAT SETTLEMENT" — MOU EXPECTED EARLY NEXT WEEK (RFERL / CBS News / CNN, June 11–12):** Trump stated from the White House that a "great settlement" with Iran had been reached and that document finalization should happen "over the next few days," with a signing potentially this weekend. Trump said he cancelled scheduled strikes because the talks had been brought to the "highest level of Iranian leadership and approved." This is the most definitive US principal-level settlement declaration of the conflict — more explicit than the May 24 "largely negotiated" or May 29 Khamenei "broad template" endorsement.
- **IRAN FM BAQAEI: "NO FINAL CONCLUSION" — NEW US DEMANDS (Euronews / CNN, June 11–12):** FM Spokesman Baqaei stated that while "most of the text" had been finalized, the US introduced new demands late in the process that broke the conclusion. He did not specify what the new demands were. Iran state TV confirmed the draft text exists. The contradiction between Trump's announcement and Baqaei's response follows the same dual-audience pattern seen at every prior deal-proximity moment; but this time both sides are simultaneously confirming the document's existence, which is new.
- **MEHR NEWS PUBLISHES DRAFT 14-POINT MOU (Arab News / Athens Times / Egyptian Gazette, June 12):** Iran's semi-official Mehr News published what it characterizes as the draft framework. Key provisions: $24B frozen assets released with $12B at signing; Hormuz reopening in exchange for US oil sanctions relief; a US-led reconstruction plan for Iran valued at "no less than $300 billion"; 60-day Phase 2 nuclear negotiations with Iran reaffirming no nuclear weapons development; Iran's missile program and support for regional proxy groups formally removed from the agreement's scope. The removal of the missile program and proxy group language from the agenda is Iran's most significant structural concession demand granted — and represents the biggest gap from Netanyahu's redlines.
- **US SHOOTS DOWN TWO IRANIAN DRONES — IRAN EXECUTED SHOOT-ON-SIGHT ORDER (RFERL, June 12):** The US military intercepted two Iranian attack drones near the Strait of Hormuz after Iranian forces reportedly fired on a transiting vessel. This confirms the June 11 "any vessel will be a target" declaration was operationally executed — not merely rhetorical — but US air defense successfully interdicted both drones. The transiting vessel was not struck. This is the first confirmed IRGC enforcement of the formal shoot-on-sight declaration; it validates the IRGC's credibility claim while the deal track simultaneously advances.
- **IRAN ACCEPTS UN REQUEST FOR HUMANITARIAN AND AGRICULTURAL SHIPMENTS (Reuters / fertilizer search results, June 12):** Iran's ambassador to the UN in Geneva confirmed Tehran has accepted a UN request to allow humanitarian aid and agricultural shipments through the Strait. This is the first formal humanitarian carve-out from the June 11 complete closure declaration — consistent with using the closure as leverage rather than executing absolute enforcement, and a partial de-escalatory signal timed to the Trump settlement announcement.
- **BRENT $87.43/bbl — DEAL-PROXIMITY RANGE ENTERED (TradingEconomics / Fortune, June 12):** Brent fell 3.26% to $87.43, its lowest level in nearly two months, entering the $85–90 deal-proximity range that has been the analytical target for S2 resolution throughout the conflict. The market is pricing approximately 55–65% deal probability at this level.

**On the Ground in Iran:** The regime is managing a difficult dual narrative: domestically, state broadcaster IRIB is simultaneously framing the deal (if it comes) as a sovereign victory — "$12B returned immediately, missile program protected, proxies protected" — while amplifying the June 12 drone execution as proof that Iran enforced its closure declaration against a US-coordinated transiting vessel. ⚠️ Iran International and Radio Farda (unverified) report that the IRGC-Vahidi military track is using the drone execution to claim institutional credit for the deal terms: "We closed the Strait, we shot at their ships, and now they are paying us $24 billion." The partial internet restoration (since May 26; still below 10% of pre-war levels; social media blocked; diaspora outlets filtered) gives the regime control over which version of the settlement narrative reaches the domestic audience. The economic backdrop remains acute: annual inflation 50.6%, cooking oil +266%, dairy +20% since June 1 (Day 12). The Mehr News publication of the 14-point draft is designed to generate both domestic legitimacy and diaspora pressure on the US to close — framing Iran not as a defeated party asking for peace but as a negotiating equal extracting concessions. The anti-war sentiment documented in student protests across 20+ cities on June 6 creates an additional pressure valve the regime needs to channel into a "we won" narrative before any settlement is signed.

**Gulf States Adaptation:** No material new development today beyond the continuing operation of existing bypass infrastructure: Saudi Arabia's east-west pipeline (approximately 7 mb/d capacity to Yanbu) and UAE's Habshan–Fujairah pipeline (approximately 1.8 mb/d to the Gulf of Oman) continue unaffected by the Hormuz closure. The Iraq al-Waleed → Syria Baniyas overland corridor remains the only Hormuz- and Red Sea-independent Gulf crude export pathway, though Iranian ballistic escalation toward Jordan's eastern geography (Al-Azraq Air Base) continues to threaten its logistics corridor. The Kirkuk–Ceyhan pipeline to Turkey's Mediterranean coast regained strategic use. The Iran humanitarian/agricultural carve-out from the UN request does not restore commercial cargo or crude tanker traffic at meaningful scale.

**Market Signals:** Drawing directly from raw data: Brent at $87.43 is the single clearest market statement of the conflict — it is pricing the deal as likely but not done, consistent with ~55–65% probability of MOU signing in the near term. I independently conclude from this level that the market has moved significantly ahead of the diplomatic text: the Baqaei contradiction and the IRGC drone execution today both confirm the gap has not closed. The deal-proximity range ($85–90) has historically been associated with S2 resolution, not a contested S3 environment. This gap between price and ground-truth suggests a 5–8% downside correction is probable if the "new US demands" gap proves structural. BDTI: last confirmed at 1,107 (June 2, Cape-route equilibrium) before the June 11 complete closure declaration. The complete closure should have driven BDTI sharply higher; I estimate the deal announcement is suppressing that repricing, leaving the index in a 1,200–1,500 estimated range as operators price deal probability against the formal shoot-on-sight order. Cape-route VLCC rates confirmed at approximately $28/MT in June by FFA forward curve data — this is normalized from the $400K+/day war-peak crisis rates, confirming the Cape diversion has fully equilibrated supply to the new routing reality. War-risk insurance remains at 2.5–5% of hull value per Lloyd's/JWC (up to $2M/VLCC voyage); crucially, Willis Towers Watson stated in May that war-risk rates "are unlikely to fall after ceasefire" because actuarial pricing resets only when years of incident-free transit data accumulate — not press conferences. The Trump DFC political risk facility ($40B revolving capacity) offsets some of this for US-linked tonnage but does not normalize commercial transit economics. Hormuz AIS transits: approximately 2 vessels on June 7 (last confirmed IMF PortWatch data), effectively zero commercially for the full closure period. The UN humanitarian carve-out may add marginal food/aid vessel transits but does not reopen crude or LNG transit at scale. **Sovereign Debt Stress:** Brent at $87.43 and the deal signal provide the first meaningful EM sovereign relief of the conflict — the December 2026 Fed rate-hike trajectory is partially offset by lower oil importing costs. However, the structural vulnerabilities remain intact: Egypt has seen approximately $6B in foreign investor withdrawals since the war started, the Egyptian pound is down 8%+ against the dollar, and the country faces more than $4B in Eurobond rollovers in the next year. Pakistan, Egypt, and Kenya face $5.1B in combined debt maturities in November–December 2026 — that cliff does not disappear with a Trump announcement, and Pakistan's mediator role has generated political goodwill but no financial relief. DXY at 99.66 (approaching the $100 level) keeps EM financial conditions tight regardless of oil price. A signed MOU with confirmed Hormuz reopening would be the most significant single EM sovereign relief event of the conflict, providing immediate relief on oil import costs and risk sentiment — but the deal is not signed. **Reconstruction Race:** The most significant new structural development in weeks: the Mehr News draft 14-point text includes a US-led reconstruction plan for Iran valued at "no less than $300 billion." This is the first formal dollar figure attached to US reconstruction engagement with Iran — and it is an order of magnitude larger than any figure previously discussed. If real, the $300B US-led reconstruction framework converts the Iran deal from a sanctions-relief transaction into a geopolitical architecture competition: whoever provides and structures $300B in post-war reconstruction financing for Iran controls Iranian economic alignment for a generation. China's second Gulf Summit GSI security architecture proposal (June 10–11) is now in direct competition with the $300B US framework for the same prize. Russia's Power of Siberia 2 commercial acceleration (Bloomberg May 19) proceeds regardless of conflict outcome. **Information Warfare:** Iran's dominant information operation today is the Mehr News publication of the draft 14-point text. By making the framework public — even as Baqaei officially contradicts Trump — Iran simultaneously achieves three audiences: domestic (we kept our missiles, our proxies, and we're getting $12B at signing — this is victory); diaspora (the deal is good; pressure the US to close it now, before they add new demands); and global south (Iran negotiated the US down from nuclear dismantlement, troop withdrawal, and proxy curtailment to a reconstruction plan and frozen asset release — this is what sovereign resistance achieves). The US information posture is Trump's "great settlement" framing — designed to claim the foreign policy win domestically regardless of timing — while CENTCOM notes the drone shoot-down as evidence of US air defense superiority rather than Iranian escalation. The "new US demands" framing from Baqaei is Iran's most effective information weapon today: it positions the US as the obstacle to peace, activates Gulf state mediators to pressure Washington, and gives the IRGC hardliner track a narrative justification for the drone execution ("we are enforcing the closure while the diplomats negotiate"). Netanyahu is losing the information war at the deal-proximity moment: Hezbollah's ceasefire veto and the Lebanon all-fronts clause — which Iran cited as its formal precondition for talks — have been effectively bypassed by both sides agreeing to exclude proxy group support from the MOU entirely.
