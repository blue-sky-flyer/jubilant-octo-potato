Confidence high. Changes if Kpler prints above 12 mb/d.

Changes on a third week.

## Model cross-check

- **Resolved — the dark-flow residual has a number.** Yesterday's cross-check named the reconciliation of CENTCOM's throughput claims against AIS as the next target. Kpler's same-day Monday figure supplies it: **3 mb/d crude measured against >19m bbl claimed**. I treat Kpler as the better estimate because it is a commercial tracker with no stake in the information fight, and because its own series moved *down* 16% week-on-week in the same period CENTCOM's claim moved up. **Both remain estimates; neither is audited.** The honest statement is that visible AIS sees ~2% of movement, Kpler claims to see ~62% of pre-war, and the belligerent claims ~140% of pre-war. The spread between the second and third is the propaganda premium.
- **Resolved — the Yanbu watch item.** Flagged Oct 14 as a test. Yanbu printed non-zero (22 kt) for the first time in three weeks. **Status: technically resolved, economically irrelevant** — it does not alter the read that the Red Sea bypass is unavailable.
- **Resolved — the Tether/coin bubble was a one-session artefact**, as the Oct 10 gold premium was. Two consecutive weeks in which a premium signal failed to persist. **Operational conclusion: single-week premium prints in Tehran instruments should be treated as noise unless they survive a second observation.** This is now a standing rule for Q3.

### Qwen
I disagree with Claude’s Q1 reading that AIS-visible flows are only ~2% of total. PortWatch shows 0.6 tankers/day (24k DWT) on Oct 11 — but Kpler’s 8.5 mb/d crude flow implies ~1.5x more tanker volume than PortWatch’s *total* tanker DWT capacity (168k DWT/week). That’s 1.7x, not 50x. The 98% dark flow claim overstates the gap. The real gap is between Kpler (commercial, likely partial) and CENTCOM’s unverified claim of 19M bbl/day — which is implausible given global daily production (~100M bbl). I assign S3 (status quo escalation) 75%, not 68%, because the *sustained* drop in Kpler flows (down 16% w/w) confirms Iran’s attacks are working — not just coercing. The MOAB target list (energy) is credible: CENTCOM’s July 9 doctrine explicitly spared export infrastructure; now targeting it is a doctrinal shift — and it’s corroborated by ISIS satellite data on hardened nuclear sites and Saudi Aramco strikes. This moves us closer to S4.
