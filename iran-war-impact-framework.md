## Daily Briefing — 2026-05-29

**Overall situation:** Day 91 / Week 13. The conflict crossed into territory it has not occupied before: US and Iranian negotiators have reached a draft 60-day ceasefire extension and gradual synchronized Hormuz reopening agreement, but both principals — Trump and Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei — have not yet approved the text. Vance told reporters the president "will be in a position where he can endorse the agreement, but obviously that's still TBD." The VIX fell to 15.74, the lowest reading of the conflict, confirming markets are pricing near-term resolution as the central case — while the IRGC simultaneously fired warning shots at four vessels attempting Hormuz transit "without authorization," demonstrating that Iran's military apparatus is enforcing its claimed maritime regime right up to the moment a deal is or is not signed.

**Key developments:**
- **TENTATIVE 60-DAY DEAL REACHED — PRINCIPALS HAVEN'T APPROVED YET**: US and Iranian negotiators have reached a draft memorandum of understanding calling for a 60-day ceasefire extension, synchronized and gradual Hormuz reopening under which Iran loosens its grip on the waterway in parallel with the US pulling back its naval blockade, and the launch of Phase 2 nuclear negotiations during the 60-day window. Vance confirmed the president is "not there yet" but will be "in a position to endorse." Trump has not publicly commented. Khamenei has not signaled approval. The $24B frozen assets sequencing dispute — which collapsed Doha — is either resolved in the draft text or deferred; specific sequencing terms have not been publicly disclosed. (Axios, CNN, ABC News — May 28, 2026)
- **US TREASURY SANCTIONS "PERSIAN GULF STRAIT AUTHORITY" — WARNS OMAN EXPLICITLY**: Treasury Secretary Bessent sanctioned Iran's PGSA institution directly, calling it "a joke" and confirming the US "will not tolerate any effort to impose a tolling system in the Strait of Hormuz." He explicitly warned Oman that any entity — directly or indirectly — facilitating Hormuz tolls will face US Treasury sanctions. Oman confirmed to Bessent it has no plans to participate in Iran's tolling scheme. This is the most direct US institutional action against the PGSA since Iran launched it on May 21 and it directly targets the state bureaucratic claim over Hormuz passage rights that Iran built as a deal-complicating structural fact. (Al Jazeera, The National, gCaptain — May 28, 2026)
- **IRGC FIRES WARNING SHOTS AT FOUR VESSELS**: IRGC Navy fired warning shots at four vessels attempting to transit Hormuz "without prior coordination or authorization." Two were stopped; two were forced to turn back. PressTV simultaneously claimed 26 vessels transited "safely" under IRGC escort. ⚠️ The 26-vessel transit claim is unverified and consistent with the May 20 IRGC information operation pattern (IMF PortWatch confirmed 0% commercial at that time); independent trackers have not confirmed any increase in commercial transits. The shooting-at-vessels-while-negotiating pattern is textbook Path C: enforces the PGSA institutional claim visibly, preserves IRGC hardliner credibility, and tests whether the deal text requires dismantling enforcement before signature. (Mehr News, CNN, Pravda EN — May 28–29, 2026)

**On the Ground in Iran:** Ground-truth reporting from inside Iran remains constrained. The internet remains partially restored at below 10% of pre-shutdown levels, social media blocked, WhatsApp restricted — the regime's curated channel is the only information pipeline to domestic audiences. No new major protest events confirmed today via BBC Persian, Radio Farda, or Iran International; prior days' scattered demonstrations (pensioners in Shush, bakers in Kermanshah, students in Khorramabad) were each dispersed with tear gas and batons per NCRI. Confirmed structural pressures continue: dairy prices are set to rise 20% from June 1 as raw milk costs surge 29%; gross fixed capital formation is −4.3% in the last reported period; bread and cereals are up 140%, red meat and poultry up 135%, oil and fats up 219%, dairy up 116.8% versus March 2025–March 2026 (IFPRI/WFP). ⚠️ The IRGC warning shots at four vessels while deal negotiations are in their most critical window reflects a domestic audience management function as much as a military one: the regime cannot be seen to simply yield the Strait while a deal is unsigned.

**Gulf States Adaptation:** The most significant Gulf development today is Oman's explicit separation from the PGSA toll scheme — Muscat confirmed directly to Treasury Secretary Bessent that it has no plans to participate in Iran's tolling mechanism, which had been the central concern of Iran's leaked "Iran-Oman joint Strait management" draft. This effectively dismantles the Iran-Oman Strait co-management model before it could be tested and removes one of the key elements of the Iranian state TV "draft deal" leak from May 27. Iraq's al-Waleed → Baniyas overland crude route remains operational, providing Iraq with a structural Mediterranean market bypass independent of the deal. Broader GCC infrastructure momentum — IMEC corridors, GCC Rail — has accelerated, with UAE, Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, Kuwait, Qatar, and Iraq all committing capital to Hormuz-bypass routes.

**Information Warfare:** The US made its most aggressive information play of the past week today: Bessent calling Iran's PGSA "a joke" while formally sanctioning it is designed for three audiences simultaneously — reassuring Israel and congressional hawks that the tentative deal does not legitimize Iran's claimed Strait authority; signaling to Oman and Gulf states that the post-deal governance framework will not include Iran's toll regime; and framing the impending deal as a US victory over Iranian institutional overreach rather than a compromise with it. Iran's information architecture is running the opposite play: IRGC warning shots at four vessels reinforces the domestic narrative that Iran is still enforcing its maritime regime, while the unverified 26-vessel "safe transit" claim extends the May 20 pattern of asserting regulatory reality through repetition. For Global South audiences, the 91-day "Iran held Hormuz and forced the US to a deal" narrative is intact and will be the dominant framing regardless of what the final text says. The IRGC's ability to fire on vessels while a deal is unsigned keeps its hardliner credentials credible domestically even if Khamenei ultimately approves.
