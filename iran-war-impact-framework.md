## Daily Briefing - 2026-10-20

**Day 235 / Week 34.** The Red Sea tanker corridor has quietly re-normalised — Bab el-Mandeb tanker tonnage is back within 5% of its 2025 level — on the same day Brent printed a five-month high. The premium is now attached to attack tempo and the Saudi front, not to aggregate seaborne logistics.

### The calls — where the read moved

**Q7 · Houthi front — the corridor has not merely stopped deteriorating; for tankers it has re-opened, and the damage has migrated entirely onshore.**
Bab el-Mandeb, week to Oct 18: 26.4 transits/day, **10.0 tankers, 554k DWT/day**, against 28.3/8.6/**436k** the prior week and 36/11/580k in 2025 (PortWatch). Tanker DWT is **−5% vs 2025**, from −39% two weeks ago; +27% w/w. Suez corroborates independently: 841k DWT/day, **+31% w/w and +8% vs 2025 on *fewer* tanker transits** (15.9 vs 17.0) — average tanker size 37.8k → 52.9k DWT. **Inference (medium): laden crude and product carriers have returned to the Suez–Red Sea route; war-risk pricing has separated spot tanker owners from liner and dry-bulk, where total transits remain −27% vs 2025.** Onshore the war intensified: 53 killed at a Saudi-backed Yemeni base ([Haaretz, Oct 19](https://www.haaretz.com/middle-east-news/2026-10-19/ty-article/houthi-missile-attack-on-saudi-backed-yemeni-army-base-kills-53/000001a1-5419-d46e-a1e5-749d28b30000)); airlines still cancelling Saudi services ([Reuters factbox, Oct 20](https://www.centraloregondaily.com/news/consumer/factbox-airlines-cancel-flights-to-saudi-arabia-after-riyadh-airport-attacks/article_c03d646e-860f-5988-9e37-15a324299af6.html)). Confidence medium-high on the data, one week only. Reverses if tanker DWT falls back under ~450k next week.

**Q1 · Flows — the war premium finally has a sell-side number, $25, and it sits ~$12 below the forward curve.**
Brent **$110.10, +0.50%** at 03:19 GMT, after $110.70 in the prior session, the highest since May 20 ([PUKmedia/Reuters, Oct 20](https://www.pukmedia.com/EN/Details/83173)). Goldman Sachs puts the war-risk premium at **nearly $25/bbl above fair value**, raises December to **$90** from $85 and sees $80 in 2027 on Gulf export recovery ([investinglive, Oct 19](https://investinglive.com/commodities/goldman-sachs-sees-25-war-risk-premium-in-brent-forecasts-90-in-december-and-80-in-2027)). **Arithmetic: $110 − $25 = ~$85 fair value; but the Jan-27 contract traded $102.59 — Goldman is roughly $12 under the curve at its own horizon.** Meanwhile Hormuz AIS tanker tonnage halved again to **13k DWT/day**: 91k DWT a week is less than a third of one VLCC, and Oct 17 recorded **zero transits of any type**. The visible series has lost resolution; Gulf volume cannot fall further in it. Confidence medium. Changes if a second bank publishes a materially different premium decomposition.

## Model cross-check

- **Resolved — the Tether premium, definitively.** The Oct 18 +0.1% print is now two weeks behind a −1.0% and a −1.2% reading, and today's came with a 3% weekly depreciation in the rial. A demand-driven squeeze cannot produce a widening discount into a falling currency. The supply-side read on Q3 is re-verified at high confidence and the promotion rule (two weeks above +1%) is retired as a live watch item until the basis crosses par again.
- **Resolved — the Munir visit.** Yesterday's watch item (1) is confirmed by Reuters via JPost for today, Oct 20. The border reopening half of that item is **not** resolved: no dated source reports Taftan–Mirjaveh or Gabd–Rimdan reopening.
- **New, not adopted — "breakthrough could be announced this week."** The New Arab, via unnamed sources familiar with mediation, relayed by Iran International. Single-originator, anonymous, and contradicted inside the same article by two named analysts. Logged, excluded from scenario weights.
- **Data integrity — the PortWatch port file.** Zero calls at Ningbo and Ulsan in the same week is impossible; the chokepoint series (which advanced normally to Oct 18) is unaffected and is what today's Q7 and Q1 calls rest on. **Caveat: if next run restores the port file with revised figures, the Oct 10–16 week should be read fresh, not differenced against today's zeros.**
- **Unresolved — the Bab el-Mandeb normalisation's cause.** Three candidate explanations: Houthi target reallocation to Saudi land infrastructure; larger laden vessels accepting the war-risk premium as the Cape detour cost rises; or a one-week AIS artefact of the same family as the port file. The Suez corroboration argues against the third, but one week is one week.
- **Watch for contradiction next run:** (1) whether BAM tanker DWT holds above 500k; (2) whether Hormuz prints a second zero-transit day; (3) whether anything dated attaches a venue to Qatar's on-the-record mediation claim.

### Qwen
I disagree with Claude’s read on Q1: the $25 war premium is not supported. Goldman Sachs’ $25 is a *model estimate*, not a market-implied premium. Brent is at $110.10, but Goldman’s own fair value of ~$85 is inconsistent with the Dec ’26 futures at $102.59 — implying a *market* premium of ~$17.50, not $25. The $25 figure is speculative, not observed. Further, Hormuz tanker DWT at 13k/day is consistent with a *near-total collapse*, not a “lost resolution.” Zero transits on Oct 17 confirm active suppression, not data noise. The premium is driven by Saudi infrastructure attacks (Khamis Mushayt, Riyadh airport) and Houthi escalation — not just Hormuz. Claude conflates Goldman’s projection with market pricing. The data supports a $15–20 premium, not $25.
