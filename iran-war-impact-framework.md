**Overall situation:** Day 121 / Week 18. The central question of June 28 is whether the Burgenstock monitoring and dispute-resolution working group — which convened today for technical-level talks scheduled through June 29 — will adjudicate the M/V Ever Lovely PGSA clearance binary before either side adds a fourth leg to the kinetic exchange cycle. **Key developments:**
- **BURGENSTOCK TECHNICAL TALKS CONVENE JUNE 28-29 (SWI / STL News):** Expert-level negotiations at Burgenstock began today with the monitoring and implementation working group convening for its first formal session — the institutional architecture doing exactly what it was designed to do, converting a kinetic exchange that destroyed the Doha MOU in May into a dispute adjudication process. The WG's ruling on IMO-corridor versus PGSA route authority is the single most consequential near-term binary for scenario probabilities: if the WG accepts the IMO corridor as MOU-compliant, CENTCOM's June 26 response was legitimate and Iran's "MOU violation" allegation fails; if the WG accepts PGSA route supremacy as embedded in the MOU's "under Iranian arrangements" language, CENTCOM struck Iran without valid cause under the treaty framework and S3 escalates toward 25%.
- **PGSA CONFIRMS M/V EVER LOVELY WAS ON UNAUTHORIZED ROUTE (STL News / GCaptain / Seatrade Maritime):** The Persian Gulf Strait Authority posted that "passage outside PGSA-designated routes" carries no safe passage guarantee and that insurance coverage is withdrawn for unauthorized routes — effectively confirming the Ever Lovely was on the IMO/Musandam Peninsula corridor without PGSA clearance when the IRGC struck it. This resolves the central factual binary: the IRGC enforced its declared route rules against an unauthorized vessel rather than arbitrarily striking an MOU-compliant ship. The legal question the WG must now answer is harder: the IMO corridor was backed by Iran's own FM and the US in the June 23 dual-corridor framework — does the MOU's Hormuz reopening language supersede PGSA route authority, or does PGSA administrative control survive inside the MOU's "Iranian arrangements" language?

Qatar MoD confirmed Al Udeid was struck with zero casualties and no significant damage; no UAE MoD confirmation of Al Dhafra as of update time. The expansion to Qatar and UAE — both previously treated as off-limits in the conflict — represents a qualitative escalation of the IRGC's Gulf targeting footprint regardless of kinetic outcome; prior deterrence assumptions about IRGC restraint toward non-US-flagged Gulf states no longer hold.
- **IRGC "HELL" WARNING + TRUMP "COMPLETE THE JOB" (Haaretz / RFE/RL, June 27-28):** IRGC issued an explicit forward threat that US bases will "experience Hell in coming days" — the most direct forward escalation warning since the June 2 "scale and nature" statement. Trump simultaneously threatened to "Complete the Job" on Iran, echoing his April-May maximum-pressure posture before the May 19 Gulf monarch intervention. The reciprocal escalatory rhetoric creates a self-reinforcing dynamic: each statement incentivizes the other side to signal resolve before the Burgenstock WG can issue a stabilizing ruling. As of this update, neither Trump nor Iran's FM has followed through with kinetic action or formal MOU annulment since the June 27 Gulf base strikes.
