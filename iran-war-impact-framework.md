**Key developments:**
- **HORMUZ TRANSIT SURGE — 32 VESSELS ON JUNE 20, FIRST MEANINGFUL COMMERCIAL OPENING (AIS / Search data, June 20):** Thirty-two vessels transited the Strait of Hormuz on June 20 — 17 inbound and 15 outbound — with 11 dark transits (8 inbound, 3 outbound). This is approximately 8x the prior AIS-visible baseline of 4/day and the highest single-day count since early in the conflict. Traffic mix remains heavily skewed toward Chinese and Indian state operators; Western commercial flags are largely absent. Two Shipping Corporation of India VLCCs (each 333m, loaded at Abu Dhabi during the conflict) coordinated departure after weeks waiting at anchor. The dark transit rate of 34% (11 of 32) reflects AIS suppression for sanctions-avoidance reasons, not mine threat — these vessels are transiting but suppressing position to avoid US secondary sanctions exposure. This is the single most significant physical Hormuz development since the Versailles signing, though it falls far short of the pre-war 94/day baseline.
- **LLOYD'S AND CHUBB LAUNCH £316M WAR-RISK CONSORTIUM FOR HORMUZ — AVAILABLE JUNE 19 (Lloyd's press release / Reinsurance News / Insurance Business, June 19):** Lloyd's and Chubb have created a new marine war-risk insurance consortium providing up to £316M (~$400M) in dedicated Hormuz capacity: $200M for hull and P&I, $200M for cargo. Available to brokers and clients from June 19, 2026. Premiums remain at 1.5-8% of hull value (reduced from peak of 2.5-5% at hull; "nexus" vessels still at the higher end). Lloyd's JWC conflict-zone designation for the Arabian Gulf remains unchanged — the consortium provides capacity within the current conflict-zone framework, not a JWC reclassification. This is a necessary market-normalization step: Western commercial operators previously had no market to buy coverage at any price; they now do. But the actuarial reset clock cannot start until the PGSA permit system's operational clearance rate is established.
- **LEBANON ESCALATION AND CEASEFIRE RENEWAL (Britannica / NPR / Times of Israel, June 19):** Hezbollah killed four Israeli soldiers overnight into June 19 — the deadliest single Hezbollah action since the Versailles signing. Israel responded with strikes on dozens of Hezbollah targets across southern Lebanon; Lebanon's Ministry of Public Health reported 47 killed. Hours later, Israel and Hezbollah agreed to renew the ceasefire on June 19. The pattern (Hezbollah provocation → large Israeli strike → ceasefire renewal) is the same cycle that collapsed and renewed four times since March. Each cycle increases the political cost for Iran of resuming formal implementation talks without resolution of the Lebanon clause.
- **BURGENSTOCK TALKS STILL UNSCHEDULED — US ENVOY HEADING TO SWITZERLAND (CNN / RTE, June 20-21):** VP Vance's June 19 Burgenstock withdrawal has not been reversed. Switzerland "remains ready to facilitate." A US envoy is still traveling to Switzerland; Iran's FM says Iran plans to meet the US "in coming days." The deal framework is stressed but neither side has formally withdrawn. The absence of a fixed date and venue for Phase 2 implementation talks is the primary implementation-track risk now that the Versailles signing is complete.

Northern route via Larak Island only; alternatives prohibited. The first test of processing times — how quickly Iran's PGSA clears applications from non-Chinese, non-Indian Western operators — will be visible within 48-72 hours of Monday June 22.

Houthi operations remain a structural residual risk to Red Sea/Bab el-Mandeb shipping for vessels avoiding Hormuz. No new Houthi attack reported in the June 20-21 window.
