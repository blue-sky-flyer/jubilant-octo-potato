## Daily Briefing - 2026-09-19

**Day 204 / Week 30.** The AIS layer has stopped measuring the Gulf: visible tanker tonnage through Hormuz is roughly 4% of what Saudi loadings alone require, so every "transits collapsed" headline is now a statement about transponders, not barrels. Meanwhile the chokepoint the consensus wrote off — Bab el-Mandeb — is running at 61% of last year's tanker tonnage, and Suez had its busiest month since December 2023.

### The calls — where the read moved

**Q1 · Hormuz dark flow — the visible tanker layer measures ~4% of actual movement; PortWatch Hormuz is now a vessel-count proxy, not a volume measurement.**
Week to Sep 13: 1.2 tankers/day, **17k DWT/day**, −99% vs 2025's 1.53M DWT/day (PortWatch). Against that, satellite-derived reporting has Saudi Arabia moving **2.8 mb/d through Hormuz over six days** plus 60M bbl sold ship-to-ship ([Srpske/Trading Economics, Sep 19](https://srpske.rs/en/news/ekonomija/2026/09/19/brent-crude-falls-european-gas-tops-80-euros); single-sourced). 2.8 mb/d ≈ 378 kt/day of cargo ≈ 420k DWT/day of laden transits. 17/420 ≈ 4%. Halve the Saudi figure and it is still under 10%. Corroboration: the one vessel the IRGC named is the *Trend*, **7,119 dwt** ([Splash247, Sep 18](https://splash247.com/iran-claims-fresh-hormuz-tanker-strike-italy-readies-red-sea-escorts)) — what remains AIS-visible is small coastal product tonnage. Confidence: medium-high on direction, low on the multiple. Changes on a Kpler laden-departure series for the Gulf.

**Q7 · Bab el-Mandeb — "functional collapse" is wrong. The Houthi seizure erased the Hormuz-diversion growth; it did not close the strait.**
PortWatch week to Sep 13: BEM **27.0 transits/day, 7.4 tankers, 405k DWT/day** — tanker DWT −39% vs 2025, −11% w/w. Suez 36.6/day, 684k DWT, −16% vs 2025. Lloyd's List Intelligence: August BEM 1,165 voyages, **+6% YoY but −49% MoM**; Suez 1,232 transits, +28% YoY, busiest since Dec 2023; 39 ultra-large boxships through BEM since July; still 70% below pre-crisis ([Lloyd's List, Sep 18](https://www.lloydslistintelligence.com/resources/blog/red-sea-brief-18-september-2026)). Read: the September coastal advance killed the *increment*, not the corridor. Confidence high (two independent series agree). Changes on a Houthi attack against a non-Saudi-affiliated vessel, or Italian national convoys drawing fire.

**Q3 · Capital flows — the Tehran gold-coin premium is approximately zero. This is a managed bleed, not a run.**
Emami coin 2,320M IRR at 2,279,000 IRR/USD = **$1,018**; the coin holds 7.32g fine gold, worth ~$1,014 at world gold ~$4,310/oz — premium ≈ **0%**. 18k gram 233.6M IRR = $102.5/g vs ~$104 world, a small *discount*. Tether at 2,273,750 sits **0.2% below** the cash dollar — no crypto exit premium. Rial flat day-on-day, ~14% weaker than the ~2.0M cited in late August (tgju). Inference (medium confidence): households liquidating hard assets to fund consumption are absorbing flight demand, so the entire adjustment runs through the exchange rate. Changes on a coin premium >10% or Tether >3% over cash.

## Model Cross-Check — differences from the core brief
*Each model below read the same signals and evidence pack and Claude's briefing, then noted where its conclusions differ. Challengers are a cross-check, not the published analysis.*

### Gemini
I largely agree with the dark-fleet and currency reads, but differ on two key calls:

* **Bab el-Mandeb read relies on lagged data:** Declaring the corridor open based on August Lloyd's List figures and PortWatch data through Sep 13 is an acute temporal error. The Houthi offensive seizing Mokha and Perim Island culminated Sep 10–12 (*The Media Line*). Lags in AIS reporting mean post-seizure commercial interdiction is not yet captured.
* **De-escalation scenario shift is unjustified:** Raising S2 (+1pp to 4%) and lowering S3 (75%) misinterprets low-sample noise (2 vs 4 Israeli Home Front alerts) while ignoring severe escalatory shocks:
  * The IRGC’s Sep 18 strike on tanker *Trend* (*Splash247*).
  * Three East-West pipeline pumping stations confirmed disabled for 3–5 weeks (*Reuters*, Sep 17).
  * Saudi Arabia cutting October crude deliveries to European refiners to zero (*Middle East Eye*, Sep 18).

S3 should remain at ≥76%, with risk tilting toward S4 (regional escalation), not S2 diplomacy.

### DeepSeek-V3
I largely concur with Claude's analysis but flag three nuanced discrepancies:

1. **Hormuz Flow Dynamics**: While data shows only 4% of baseline tanker tonnage visible via AIS (PortWatch), the claim that this implies 96% is moving "dark" is overstated. Alternate transit routes (e.g., trucking via Turkey per FT) and collapsed Iranian exports (zero Kharg Island calls for 3 weeks) mean actual seaborne volumes are likely higher than AIS suggests but lower than pre-war levels. The 96% gap isn't solely spoofing/dark shipping.

2. **Bab el-Mandeb Resilience**: The 61% YoY tanker tonnage (vs. 2025) understates current fragility. Houthi control of Perim Island (seized Sep 10) introduces new asymmetric threats not captured in aggregate transit counts. Lloyd’s List notes *ultra-large containerships* now dominate BEM traffic—harder to interdict but more disruptive if attacked. The "functional collapse" framing was wrong, but the risk profile has degraded.

3. **Rial Stability Overstated**: The 0% gold/Tether premium masks severe distortions. Nobitex exchange volumes (crypto) fell 89% YoY per Treasury sanctions filings—liquidity has evaporated, making nominal stability meaningless. The 14% Rial drop since August aligns with IMF inflation projections of 1,200% annualized, implying *accelerating* depreciation despite reported "flat" daily rates (IODA/tgju).

### Qwen
I differ on Q1: Claude assumes Saudi’s 2.8 mb/d through Hormuz is credible, but PortWatch shows only 1.2 tankers/day (17k DWT) — a 99% drop — and no satellite data confirms this volume. The 2.8 mb/d claim (Srpske) is uncorroborated by AIS, Kpler, or tanker tracking firms, and contradicts Kharg Island’s zero tanker calls and Ras Tanura’s exports down 70% YoY. This isn’t “dark flow” — it’s likely a speculative narrative. I set S3 (status quo) at 70%, not 75%, because the Houthi seizure of Bab el-Mandeb islands and Saudi pipeline damage (Reuters, Sep 17) are escalating, not stabilizing, pressure. The 22 U.S. troop deaths (WaPo) and Trump’s “annihilate” remark (Axios, Sep 17) signal higher escalation risk than Claude acknowledges.

### Llama 3.3
I largely agree with Claude's briefing. However, I have a few differences:

* **Inference on tanker traffic**: While the reported tanker traffic through Hormuz is significantly lower than last year, I believe the actual traffic may be higher due to the use of unreported and unmonitored routes. This could lead to an underestimation of the true supply and demand balance in the region.
* **Red Sea shipping insurance**: The recent attacks on Saudi Arabia and the increased tensions in the Red Sea may lead to higher shipping insurance costs, which could further exacerbate the supply chain disruptions and increase the risks of shipping through the region.
* **Impact of Houthi advances on global oil prices**: The Houthi advances in Yemen and the subsequent closure of the East-West pipeline may have a more significant impact on global oil prices than initially thought, as this could lead to a longer-term reduction in oil exports from Saudi Arabia.

These differences may affect the overall assessment of the situation, but I generally concur with Claude's analysis.
