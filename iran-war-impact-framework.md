The original levy — which would have taxed all US-escorted Hormuz transits at 20% of cargo value — had no precedent in international maritime law and was drawing public opposition from European allies and US shipping interests. The reversal is a minor S3-constructive signal: it removes the most legally confrontational new element introduced this week and suggests the administration is sensitive to allied pressure on the maritime economics dimension. The naval blockade on Iranian ports (in effect since July 14 at 4pm ET) remains unchanged.

The incident confirms Iran's targeting doctrine has expanded from deterrence to attrition: sequential attacks across vessel nationalities (Qatari LNG, Saudi crude, UAE supertankers) to enforce PGSA route supremacy irrespective of flag-state.

**Information Warfare:** Iran's "national interests" talks signal today is a well-calibrated information operation: it reduces international pressure on Tehran as the party refusing dialogue (reversing the image from Iran-as-MOU-suspender to Iran-as-willing-to-talk), while committing Iran to nothing specific. Trump's bridge-and-power-plant threat, telegraphed "next week" from mid-July, creates a humanitarian-harm narrative before execution — Tehran will exploit any civilian infrastructure strike globally. The 20% levy reversal hands Iran a counter-narrative win (US threat retracted under allied pressure) without any Iranian concession. CENTCOM's "weekend wave" language subtly signals US operational restraint — potentially the first US information-operation softening since the MOU collapsed. Net assessment: Iran is winning the information-warfare phase today on multiple dimensions (talks signal, levy walk-back, civilian infrastructure victim-framing from Kuwait) even as it loses the kinetic dimension.
