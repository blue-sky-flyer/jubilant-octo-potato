**S2 falls 2pp (→7%):** The UAE's financial-transaction suspension with Iran is qualitatively distinct from prior Gulf-state responses — it is a unilateral economic severance that further narrows Iran's financial operating space and signals GCC willingness to escalate outside the US-led framework. Combined with Operation Economic Fury, the compounding economic pressure reduces the probability of a near-term negotiated off-ramp: Iran's "military aggression" framing of new economic measures signals the IRGC is treating economic escalation as a casus belli, not a negotiating pressure point. S2 now requires both a reversal of this framing and an Omani or alternative interlocutor publicly confirmed — neither is in evidence.

**S4 rises 1pp (→13%):** Operation Economic Fury — if it targets Iran's remaining financial lifelines (dark-fleet insurance, CIPS-alternative settlement channels, third-country intermediaries) — compresses Iran's fiscal position faster than the siege-endurance trajectory modeled in the Disintegration Tracker. A faster fiscal collapse without a diplomatic off-ramp increases the probability of IRGC self-authorization of escalatory options, including nuclear-security-pretext actions, particularly given the succession vacuum (no authorized treaty signatory). The ISW/NYT double-sourced finding that IRGC coordinated expanded attack plans during the MOU period reinforces that the IRGC is in active escalation-preparation mode, not waiting for political authorization.

**S5 rises 1pp (→4%):** The UAE financial-transaction suspension, if it triggers Chinese financial institution exposure to US secondary sanctions (UAE is a major CIPS clearing hub for Iran-linked transactions), raises the probability of a direct US-China financial confrontation. Three China-linked supertankers turning back from Hormuz on Aug 20 suggests Chinese operators are self-sanctioning ahead of Operation Economic Fury specifics — but if the new measures target Chinese intermediaries directly, Beijing's domestic-law anti-foreign-sanctions reflex (invoked May 2026) could activate.

### Qwen

1. **Iran’s “military aggression” threat is not credible as a casus belli** — Sputnik (via Times of India) is a single, uncorroborated source. The three Chinese tankers turning back reflect self-sanctioning, not U.S.-China confrontation risk — Beijing has no formal financial ties to Iran’s oil exports, and CIPS transactions are minimal.
