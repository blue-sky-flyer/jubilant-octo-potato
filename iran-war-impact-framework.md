### The calls — where the read moved

**Q1 · Flows — Kharg has started loading, and the blockade line is not at Kharg.**
PortWatch records Kharg at **1 tanker call and 295 kt of exports** in the week to Nov 20, against zero the prior week and zero in the same 2025 week (PortWatch). *Agent arithmetic: 295 kt ≈ **2.16m bbl** at 7.33 bbl/t — eleven times the 200,000-bbl Olym→Hao Fan ship-to-ship transfer of Nov 17, so this is a terminal loading, not that transfer re-counted.* It contradicts the Treasury position that Iranian loadings stopped on Aug 25. It does not contradict UANI, which still records **no crude-laden tanker clearing the Gulf of Oman since Jul 12** ([UANI Nov 20](https://www.unitedagainstnucleariran.com/analysis/iran-shipping-update-november-20-2026)). Caveat: PortWatch tonnage is modelled from hull capacity, so a call can generate the estimate without a departure. Meanwhile Hormuz tanker DWT fell 18k → 1k/day (−94%), closing last week's +476% artefact. Confidence medium. Changes on an interdiction of a laden hull, or a Kharg cargo discharging in Asia.

**Q2 · Inside Iran — the vice-president's blockade claim is true for the Caspian and false for the system.**
Mohammad Reza Aref: increased clearance "through non-southern borders sends the message that we are not concerned about the enemy's naval blockade" ([Reuters via USNews Nov 25](https://www.usnews.com/news/world/articles/2026-11-25/iran-says-it-is-prepared-to-withstand-us-blockade-seeks-to-curb-price-rises)). The system does not: Iranian Gulf imports 345 → 213 kt, Gulf calls 30 → 21, and **total AIS-visible seaborne imports 250 kt against 923 kt in the 2025 week = 27%**, −33% w/w (PortWatch, agent arithmetic). Amirabad took 22 calls for 20 kt — 0.9 kt a call against the Gulf's 10 kt. Maritime Executive bounds it: Bandar Abbas carried 80% pre-blockade and the residual channels cannot more than double, so ~40% of normal is the ceiling; Bazargan queues are down from three weeks to one ([ME Nov 25](https://maritime-executive.com/editorials/caspian-shipping-routes-into-iran-under-pressure)). Confidence high.

## Model cross-check

- **I got Brent wrong yesterday and the error was structural, not arithmetic.** Two price series in the same pack, no reconciliation step. The fix is a rule: never compute a percentage move across two sources unless both are the same instrument. The substantive consequence is that yesterday's framing — markets discounting tanker attacks — is unsupported; on the futures the attacks were being priced *in*.
- **The Kharg print is the weakest-sourced strong claim in today's briefing.** PortWatch estimates export tonnage; it does not weigh cargo. One VLCC-class call at a terminal with ~30m bbl in storage will generate a large number whether or not the ship sails. I have stated it as a loading signal, not an export, and the UANI departure record is the control. If next week's Kharg line returns to zero with no departure, this was a staging event.
- **Against my own Q2 call.** The 27%-of-2025 figure counts only AIS-visible seaborne arrivals. It cannot see trucks, and the entire Iranian claim is about trucks. Dogharoun exports to Afghanistan are reported up 32% by weight this year; the Maritime Executive ceiling arithmetic (80% × Bandar Abbas, residual doubles at best) is the strongest check I have, and it is one outlet's judgement, not a measurement.
- **Two triggers fired in the same run and I have discounted both.** Yanbu registered loadings after three zero weeks (Q7) and the Emami bubble crossed 10% (Q3) — and in each case I read the magnitude or the persistence as disqualifying. That is defensible twice; a third time it becomes a habit of refusing my own tripwires. Logged deliberately.
- **Watch for contradiction next run:** whether Kharg prints again and whether CENTCOM says anything about it; whether the Nov 29 OPEC+ ministerial moves Brent off ~$91; the Nov 26–27 Islamabad SPDC against Fidan's non-automatic language; whether Yanbu's single call becomes several; and whether the Tether premium holds above +1% for a fifth print.
