## Daily Briefing — 2026-07-10

**Overall situation:** Day 133 / Week 19. The June 18 MOU ceasefire has formally collapsed: Trump declared it "over" on July 10 while simultaneously agreeing to continue talks — a dual-track posture that leaves the conflict in an unstable active-negotiation-while-fighting phase. Qatari mediators flew to Tehran on Friday to create conditions for a next round, tentatively expected in Switzerland next week. The decisive binary is whether Iran issues a statement declaring Hormuz open — US officials said they expect this — or whether IRGC vessel strikes continue to trigger CENTCOM responses.

**Key developments:**
- **Trump declares ceasefire "OVER," talks to continue** (Axios / Bloomberg / CBS, July 10): Trump posted that the MOU ceasefire is over but Iran has asked to resume talks and the US has agreed; Qatar confirmed its mediators traveled to Tehran July 10 in coordination with Washington; a Switzerland round is expected next week; both sides continue to blame each other for breaking the MOU's Hormuz shipping clause.
- **Iran fires 10 ballistic missiles at US bases in Jordan, Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait** (Al Jazeera / Reuters, July 9): The largest single Iranian ballistic missile salvo of the conflict against US base infrastructure; no confirmed US casualties reported; CENTCOM struck additional Iranian targets in response; this escalation directly followed the July 7 IRGC vessel strikes on the Qatari LNG tanker and Saudi crude tanker.
- **US expects Iran to declare Hormuz open** (CNN senior officials, July 10): US officials say they anticipate an Iranian declaration that the strait is open as a condition for resuming negotiations; no confirmation from Tehran; traceable commercial transits remain near standstill.
- **Saudi-led mediation axis consolidates** (Foreign Policy, July 1): A new grouping of Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Turkey, Pakistan, and Egypt — notably excluding the UAE — is coordinating mediation pressure; the UAE continues to favor military confrontation; the intra-Gulf split is now structurally visible.

**On the Ground in Iran:** At least 135 protests, strikes, and demonstrations took place across Iran in June alone — the broadest sustained civil unrest since the 1979 revolution, spanning workers, pensioners, students, healthcare staff, and farmers (Iran News Update / NCRI). The framing on Telegram and diaspora channels is explicitly economic: "refrigerators are empty" (JPost interview); 40% inflation, widespread power and water cuts, and wartime conscription pressure are the dominant grievances. ⚠️ No verified July 10-specific ground reporting yet from BBC Persian or IranWire; June trajectory assumed to continue.

**Gulf States Adaptation:** Saudi Arabia is routing approximately 7 million barrels per day through its east-west pipeline to Yanbu on the Red Sea, bypassing Hormuz entirely; the UAE is exporting up to 1.8 million barrels per day via the Habshan-Fujairah pipeline to the Gulf of Oman. These two bypass pipelines represent the primary physical adaptation to the strait closure — they cover Saudi and UAE crude exports but do not help other Gulf states or cover import flows of non-oil goods. The Saudi-UAE strategic split is now operational as well as rhetorical: Saudi Arabia is coordinating mediation while the UAE has adopted a defiant posture.

Brent at $76/bbl (down slightly on day, up on week) — the market is pricing a risk premium above the pre-war ~$72 level but far below the S3 scenario range, implying the futures curve still expects Hormuz reopening within weeks not months. Hormuz transit count ~34 ships/day versus the pre-war 88+ baseline — near-standstill, not zero, which I read as: some captains are still transiting under escort or at risk, but the effective commercial closure is real. Gold $4,105, VIX 15.71, DXY 100.80 — no geopolitical bid in safe havens; markets continue to price zero S3 probability despite ballistic missile salvo. **Sovereign Debt Stress:** Egypt, Pakistan, and Kenya face $5.1 billion in combined debt maturities in November–December 2026; Egypt's $170 billion external debt (45% of GDP) is partially cushioned by a $1.6 billion IMF tranche draw from its $8 billion facility, but the currency trajectory depends entirely on the Hormuz reopening timeline. Pakistan has enacted a four-day government work week and two-week school closures to conserve fuel. The IMF revised its global growth forecast downward on July 8. Egypt is the single country closest to a sovereign stress event given its Suez Canal revenue collapse coinciding with fuel import cost surge. **Reconstruction Race:** China is the most aggressive positioner: Wang Yi pledged reconstruction aid on June 22 and Chinese firms are pre-staging BRI-linked financing structures for Iranian port, energy, and desalination infrastructure under the 25-year $400 billion oil-for-investment agreement signed in 2021. ECFR analysis frames China — not Russia — as the likely strategic winner of the war regardless of outcome. Russia is collecting windfall oil revenues ($45–161 billion additional in 2026 depending on duration) but is less focused on post-war positioning. US reconstruction leverage is constrained by its insistence on nuclear concessions as a precondition for sanctions relief. See [reference doc](https://github.com/blue-sky-flyer/jubilant-octo-potato/blob/main/iran-war-reference.md).

**Information Warfare:** Iran is winning the "vibe war" in Global South audiences through AI-driven propaganda and narrative framing that positions the US as a destabilizing energy aggressor (Foreign Affairs Forum). The IRGC is simultaneously intensifying diaspora suppression — monitoring journalists, threatening families, and running cyber-surveillance operations against opposition figures abroad (Foreign Policy, July 7). The regime frames domestic protests as Mossad/CIA-orchestrated; the diaspora counter-narrative centers on Reza Pahlavi and monarchist restoration — gaining traction but fragmented. Today the dominant narrative battle is over who broke the MOU: Iran blames US escalation; US blames IRGC vessel strikes.
