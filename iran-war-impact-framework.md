## Daily Briefing — 2026-07-14

**Overall situation:** Day 137 / Week 20. The Versailles MOU has collapsed in operational terms. Following the July 7 three-vessel IRGC enforcement cluster that drove the Doha channel to the brink, Iran and the US entered a seven-day escalation spiral that has produced the most acute military exchange since the June 10-11 cycle that preceded the original deal. Trump declared the ceasefire "OVER" on July 10. The US has now launched at least three consecutive nights of strikes on Iranian territory (July 12-13-14), targeting Bushehr, Chahbahar, Jask, Konarak, Abu Musa, and Bandar Abbas. Iran retaliated by attacking five Gulf states simultaneously with ballistic missiles and drones on July 12, striking two UAE oil supertankers in Hormuz on July 13, and firing again at Bahrain and Jordan on July 14. The naval blockade was formally reimposed at 4pm ET today. Trump simultaneously declared the US the "guardian of Hormuz" and announced a 20% cargo toll on all non-Iranian vessels transiting the Strait. Brent crude repriced from $72.89 on July 7 to $86.35 today (+18.5%). The scenario picture has inverted: S3 is now the commanding majority. Talks technically remain alive — Switzerland next week — but the structural ceasefire architecture has dissolved.

**Key developments:**
- **NAVAL BLOCKADE REIMPOSED + 20% CARGO TOLL** ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/13/trump-iran-hormuz-strait-charge-reimburse.html), [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/13/trump-says-us-will-become-guardian-of-strait-of-hormuz-and-collect-tolls), July 13-14): CENTCOM began enforcing a full naval blockade of all Iranian ports and coastal areas effective 4pm ET July 14 — the second imposition of the blockade since it was lifted as part of the June 17 Versailles MOU. All neutral vessels were warned to depart the blockaded area before enforcement began; non-compliant vessels are subject to interception, diversion, capture, and use of force. Trump simultaneously announced the US will collect a 20% fee on all cargo shipped through Hormuz "for any and all costs necessary to do the job of providing safety and security." The UN's International Maritime Organization stated Monday there is "no legal basis through which to introduce mandatory tolls" — the first direct international institution pushback on US Hormuz governance since the MOU collapsed. This is structurally distinct from the pre-Versailles blockade: the 20% toll claim represents a US assertion of permanent transit-fee sovereignty over Hormuz that directly mirrors — and competes with — Iran's own PGSA fee architecture.

Trump stated Iran "has nothing" to stop the attacks and warned of additional strikes "over the next two days." The three-night sustained strike package surpasses every prior single-sequence US strike series of the conflict, including the June 10-11 package that produced the Versailles MOU. CENTCOM released unclassified footage of the operations.

Jordan intercepted incoming Iranian fire the same day. In Kuwait, military air defenses shot down three ballistic missiles, a cruise missile, and ten drones; one person was wounded by falling debris. Bahrain shot down incoming fire; Jordan government spokesman confirmed all incoming fire intercepted. No confirmed casualties from the July 12 multi-state salvo, but the breadth — five Gulf states plus Jordan simultaneously — establishes a new conflict escalation record and directly tests all of the Gulf host-nation deterrence assumptions that were structurally reset after the June 27-28 Al Udeid/Al Dhafra expansion. Iran separately struck two UAE oil supertankers in Hormuz on July 13, resuming direct commercial shipping targeting one day before the blockade reimposition.

Iran accused the US of violating the MOU first; the US accused Iran of violating it with the July 7 vessel strikes. Both sides are maintaining a formal "talks ongoing" posture while executing full-intensity kinetic operations simultaneously — a structural contradiction that defines the July 14 negotiating landscape.

Of the 14, only four were crude oil tankers. The pre-war baseline was 120-140 vessels per day (~20 million barrels of oil). CNBC confirmed more than 8 million barrels transited Sunday (July 13) with US military assistance — the "military assistance" qualifier marks a structural shift from commercial transit to US Navy-escorted transit that did not exist under the Versailles framework. Brent crude rose to $86.35/bbl on July 14 (+3.66% on the day; +18.5% from July 7's $72.89), with a 13.7% rise recorded over the five-day period July 7-13. The market has repriced approximately half the war premium that existed at the April 30 peak ($126.41) but remains well below that level.

**On the Ground in Iran:** The combination of resumed US strikes and the naval blockade reimposition lands on an economy already in freefall. The IMF projects Iran's GDP to contract -5.4% in 2026 — the sharpest contraction since the 1979 Revolution. The Central Bank of Iran's own data shows $27 billion in capital flight in 2025 alone (>7% of GDP). Inflation officially clocked at 88.6% YoY in June; independent estimates suggest point-to-point above 100% in food categories. On July 12, retirees and citizens defied temperatures exceeding 50°C in Shush and Kermanshah to protest, chanting "Warmongering is enough, our tables are empty!" and "Inflation and high prices, no to war and destruction!" — the first confirmed protests specifically directed at the government's war posture during the post-burial return to kinetics. On July 13, defrauded investors clashed with security forces simultaneously with healthcare worker strikes in Neyshabur and telecom retiree strikes in Ahvaz and Sanandaj. The NCRI ⚠️[MEK-affiliated] compiled 135 separate protests, strikes, and demonstrations across Iran in June 2026. IRGC domestic security bandwidth is at maximum stress: the blockade reimposition will tighten fuel and food import economics already at breaking point. Mojtaba Khamenei was absent from his father's Mashhad burial on July 9, completing a 137-day absence with no confirmed audio or video; the succession vacuum remains structurally unresolved.

**Gulf States Adaptation:** Saudi Arabia's East-West Pipeline reached full 7M bpd capacity in March and remains the primary Gulf bypass architecture; the Saudi crude tanker struck in Hormuz on July 7 was transiting rather than pipeline-routing, suggesting commercial operators attempted Hormuz normalization before this week's collapse. The UAE's Habshan-Fujairah Abu Dhabi Crude Oil Pipeline (1.5M bpd, 71% utilization in March with 1.8M bpd surge capacity) provides Fujairah terminal a critical non-Hormuz exit; ADNOC announced a parallel "West-East 1" pipeline project on May 15 (operational 2027) that will double Fujairah export capacity. Kuwait and Bahrain have no bypass pipeline and remain fully Hormuz-dependent under the reimposed blockade. Qatar's LNG force majeure (extended to mid-August) remains in effect — the Al Rekayyat IRGC strike on a Qatari state LNG tanker on July 7 was a structural blow to Qatar's neutral mediator status; Qatar's role in the Swiss talks expected next week is uncertain given the prior "fully legally responsible" condemnation of Iran. Iran attacked Qatar (along with four other Gulf states) in the July 12 multi-state salvo, further straining the Doha channel architecture.

**Information Warfare:** Three simultaneous narrative battles are running on incompatible terrain. (3) The five-Gulf-state simultaneous attack narrative is the most potent international information asset for the Gulf coalition: framing Iran as an indiscriminate aggressor targeting multiple neutral sovereigns simultaneously undermines Iran's "self-defense against US aggression" framing with the non-aligned bloc. Al Jazeera English leads with the Gulf states-as-victims framing; RT/TASS leads with US strikes-on-sovereign-Iran framing — the information war is splitting cleanly along the pre-existing great-power media architecture.
