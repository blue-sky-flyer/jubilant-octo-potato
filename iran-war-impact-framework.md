## Daily Briefing — 2026-06-11

**Overall situation:** Day 104 / Week 15. Iran's joint military command formally declared the Strait of Hormuz "closed to all vessels, including tankers and merchant ships" and stated that "any movement in the Strait will be a target" — the most consequential single development since the initial Hormuz closure on February 28. This converts what has been a kinetically enforced partial disruption operating under an ambiguous PGSA toll regime into a publicly declared shoot-on-sight complete closure with no carve-out. The US simultaneously launched a second consecutive day of strikes, firing 49 Tomahawk cruise missiles at Iranian air defense systems, communications infrastructure, and radar sites across Hormozgan Province — the largest US strike package since Operation Epic Fury on February 28. Jordan's air defense intercepted approximately 20 Iranian ballistic missiles targeting Al-Azraq Air Base, Iran's highest single-country ballistic salvo of the conflict against any one state, marking Iran's second consecutive day striking Jordanian territory. US CPI for May came in at 4.2% year-on-year — the fastest pace in more than three years, driven by surging energy costs — reinforcing December Fed rate-hike pricing. The key binary: does the formal shoot-on-sight declaration represent an operational shift Iran will enforce against the next vessel, or a rhetorical ceiling designed to extract deal concessions from the US and China before the complete closure proves unenforceable?

**Key developments:**
- **IRAN DECLARES COMPLETE HORMUZ CLOSURE — "ANY VESSEL WILL BE A TARGET" (Business Standard / The Star / Tempo.co, June 11):** The Headquarters of Khatam Al-Anbiya formally announced the Strait is "closed to all vessels, including oil tankers and commercial ships" effective June 11, framing the declaration as a response to US strikes on Hormozgan Province. The IRGC stated any movement in the Strait constitutes a target. This eliminates the operational distinction between PGSA toll refusal and kinetic closure — any vessel attempting transit, regardless of prior PGSA authorization or Chinese state backing, is now declared a military target. The declaration effectively terminates the dark-transit protocol that had allowed approximately 40 vessels to exit quietly via US Navy coordination over the prior three weeks.
- **US FIRES 49 TOMAHAWK CRUISE MISSILES — SECOND CONSECUTIVE DAY OF STRIKES (CBS News / Britannica, June 11):** US Air Force and Navy assets struck Iranian air defense systems, communication nodes, and surveillance radar sites across southern Iran — firing 49 Tomahawk cruise missiles in the process. This follows the approximately 20-target strike package of June 10. Combined, the two-day US strike sequence is the most intensive US offensive action against Iran since the opening Operation Epic Fury strikes on February 28, covering the same Bandar Abbas–Sirik–Jask–Qeshm–Hormozgan theater. CENTCOM continues to characterize these as "self-defense" operations.
- **JORDAN INTERCEPTS ~20 IRANIAN BALLISTIC MISSILES AT AL-AZRAQ AIR BASE (Britannica / Wikipedia, June 11):** Jordanian air defense intercepted approximately 20 Iranian ballistic missiles targeting Al-Azraq Air Base — the highest single-country, single-session intercept count in the conflict, and Iran's second confirmed ballistic attack on Jordanian territory in 24 hours. The salvo escalation arc is stark: 2 ballistic missiles at Kuwait (June 1) → 7 missiles at Kuwait and Bahrain simultaneously (June 6) → 3-country ballistic retaliation including Jordan (June 10) → ~20 missiles at Jordan alone (June 11). Al-Azraq is in eastern Jordan near the Iraqi border, directly in the corridor of the al-Waleed → Syria Baniyas overland crude route — the conflict's only Hormuz- and Red Sea-independent Gulf crude export pathway.
- **US CPI MAY 4.2% YOY — FRESH THREE-YEAR HIGH (CBS News, June 11):** Consumer price inflation accelerated to 4.2% in May, up from 3.8% in April — the fastest pace since mid-2023, driven by surging energy costs. This cements the December 2026 Fed rate-hike trajectory, tightening financial conditions for emerging market sovereigns already under pressure from the war's energy shock.
- **BRENT $93.50 EARLY TRADING — EXTREME MISPRICE (TradingEconomics, June 11 early session):** Brent opened at $93.50 (+0.43%) before the complete Hormuz closure declaration was absorbed by markets. Prior complete-closure scenario pricing by IEA and independent analysts was $130+; a complete closure with active shoot orders should reprice Brent far above the current level, though the degree depends on whether the shoot order is executed against a vessel in the next 24-48 hours.

**On the Ground in Iran:** Iranian state broadcaster IRIB is framing the complete Hormuz closure declaration as an assertion of sovereign authority against American aggression — the regime's most powerful pro-war domestic narrative since the Apache shoot-down 48 hours prior. ⚠️ Radio Farda and Iran International report (unverified) that the IRGC is internally treating the two-day combination of Apache kill + complete closure as the conflict's most credible domestic legitimacy assertion yet, holding the thermal/optical Apache footage for synchronized release with the closure narrative for maximum combined impact. The "under attack, rally" dynamic historically suppresses explicit political protest in the 72-96 hours following major kinetic exchanges; the complete closure declaration deploys precisely when domestic economic pressure is most acute and the regime most needs a national-unity narrative to crowd out it out.

**Gulf States Adaptation:** The complete Hormuz closure declaration with shoot orders effectively terminates the dark-transit protocol — the ~40 vessels that exited quietly over three weeks via US Navy AIS-off coordination cannot safely continue under a formal "any vessel is a target" order. Saudi Arabia's east-west pipeline (approximately 7 mb/d capacity to Yanbu on the Red Sea) and the UAE's Habshan-Fujairah pipeline (approximately 1.8 mb/d to the Gulf of Oman) are structurally unaffected — both routes bypass Hormuz entirely and were built precisely for this scenario. The al-Waleed → Syria Baniyas overland corridor (Iraqi crude, modest volumes, active since May 28) is newly threatened by Iran's escalating Jordan ballistic campaign: Al-Azraq is in eastern Jordan near the Iraqi border, and a sustained Iranian ballistic focus on Jordan's eastern military geography would directly threaten the overland route's logistics corridor. The Houthi Red Sea ban on Israeli-nexus vessels (declared June 8) remains active; with Hormuz now formally closed to all vessels, the dual-chokepoint scenario is closer to execution than at any prior moment in the conflict — full Bab el-Mandab commercial execution would simultaneously block the Red Sea/Suez alternative used by non-Gulf shippers.
