## Daily Briefing — 2026-07-12

**Overall situation:** Day 135 / Week 20. The Islamabad MOU ceasefire is formally dead. In the 25 hours since the IRGC declared Hormuz closed (July 11), the war has escalated to its broadest geographic footprint: CENTCOM completed its 3rd strike wave (300+ cumulative Iranian military targets over three nights), and Iran retaliated against six Gulf states simultaneously — Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait, Jordan, the UAE, and Oman — with ballistic missiles and suicide drones. Markets have not yet opened for Monday; Brent closed Friday at ~$76.80/bbl and will almost certainly gap higher at open.

**On the Ground in Iran:** No significant new reporting beyond continued protest actions consistent with prior weeks. CENTCOM's 300+ target campaign has substantially degraded Iran's missile/drone launch infrastructure per CENTCOM's own characterization — however operational capability to continue strikes on Gulf states was demonstrated within hours of the latest US wave, suggesting significant redundancy or reserve systems remain active. Domestic fuel crisis and economic pressure continue as structural baseline.

**Gulf States Adaptation:** The UAE strike represents a fundamental change in the threat environment for the Gulf's two most critical economic anchor states (UAE and Saudi Arabia). Saudi Arabia has condemned Iran's attacks per [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2650194/middle-east) but has not announced retaliatory action or formal alliance posture. Saudi east-west pipeline (7M bbl/day to Yanbu) and UAE Habshan-Fujairah pipeline (1.8M bbl/day) remain active bypass infrastructure — but the UAE's physical security is now directly threatened. Any damage to UAE's Fujairah terminal would materially impair the bypass routing that has kept Gulf crude moving during the Hormuz closure.

**Market Signals:** Brent closed Friday at ~$76.80/bbl ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/article/price-of-oil-07-10-2026/)) — this was before Sunday's Gulf state strikes and 3rd CENTCOM wave. Monday open will be the first market read on the full escalation. Directional call: Brent gaps toward $82–88 on Monday open depending on whether the UAE's Fujairah terminal reports damage. BDTI likely moves toward 1,200+ on Monday; war-risk insurance premiums for Gulf corridor vessels expected to re-spike. ⚠️ Monday open prices unavailable at time of writing; monitor closely.

**Sovereign Debt Stress:** Egypt and Pakistan face their highest acute stress since the war began: Suez Canal revenues collapse with Hormuz closure (fewer LNG/crude cargoes to transit via the Cape bypass that would otherwise route through Suez), and Pakistan's fuel import cost spike is now structural. Jordan's direct involvement (missiles struck Jordanian territory for third time) creates new political instability risk for Amman. No new IMF action reported; the tipping point is approaching.

**Reconstruction Race:** The 300+ CENTCOM strike campaign represents the largest single infrastructure destruction event since the February 28 opening strikes. China's reconstruction leverage over Iran increases with each strike wave — Wang Yi's June 22 pledge is now the largest firm reconstruction commitment on the table. The Swiss roadmap's IAEA provisions are suspended but not withdrawn; Beijing may quietly attempt to keep the IAEA access track alive to preserve optionality.

**Information Warfare:** Iran's simultaneous attack on six Gulf states is a deliberate "widening the war" information move — forcing every Gulf state to publicly choose a side and demonstrating that no Gulf neighbor is neutral in a US-Iran conflict. The UAE strike in particular is designed to fracture the GCC's posture: Abu Dhabi has been the most US-aligned Gulf capital and striking it sends a message to Riyadh about the cost of alignment. Counter-signal: Iran attacking Oman (its traditional back-channel partner) may be a mistake — damages the Araghchi-Muscat diplomatic track that was Iran's only active negotiating lifeline.
