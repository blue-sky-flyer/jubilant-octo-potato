## Daily Briefing — 2026-06-13

**Overall situation:** Day 106 / Week 16. Pakistan's Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif declared on June 12 that a "final, agreed upon text" has been reached between the US and Iran, and that a deal was "less than 24 hours away" (Al Jazeera). Trump escalated this framing by claiming on June 13 that Khamenei had "personally approved" the agreement; Iran's Foreign Ministry did not confirm it, and Iran's FM spokesman continued to cite "new US demands." JD Vance was named as the US signatory, with signing expected "over the weekend in Europe" — Geneva named as likely venue. The deal is not yet signed. The key unresolved structural issue: Iran's "service fees" compromise — the Persian Gulf Strait Authority survives in rebranded form as a "navigational services fee" mechanism rather than a toll, meaning the institution itself is not dismantled by the MOU and Hormuz does not return to pre-war conditions. The deal's critical binary is whether this semantic compromise satisfies the draft MOU's "unrestricted passage" language — and whether Netanyahu acts in the 24–96 hours before Vance boards a flight to Geneva.

**Key developments:**
- **PAKISTAN PM: "FINAL, AGREED UPON TEXT" — DEAL "LESS THAN 24 HOURS AWAY" (Al Jazeera / WaPo / NBC News, June 12–13):** PM Shehbaz Sharif announced that the US and Iran had agreed on final deal wording, with Pakistan having worked to finalize next steps. This is the most concrete third-party principal confirmation of a completed text in the conflict — more operationally specific than prior "close" or "work in progress" language from US officials. Regional officials expect a signing ceremony "in the coming days" after Washington and Tehran formally approve.
- **TRUMP: KHAMENEI "PERSONALLY APPROVED" — IRAN FM DENIES (June 13):** Trump claimed that Iran's Supreme Leader Khamenei had personally approved the agreement — a claim Iran's Foreign Ministry did not confirm, with the FM again citing "new US demands" as complicating final closure. The claim is structurally significant as an information operation: it publicly commits Khamenei to the deal without his acknowledgment, creating a domestic validation crisis inside Iran — denying it makes him look anti-peace, accepting it forces early signing.
- **VANCE NAMED TO SIGN IN GENEVA "OVER THE WEEKEND" (NBC News / search results, June 12–13):** JD Vance has been designated as the US signatory for the MOU, with Geneva floated as the venue and signing expected over the weekend of June 13–15. This is the most operationally specific signing timeline of the conflict — a named principal, a named city, and a named window.
- **"SERVICE FEES" COMPROMISE — PGSA SURVIVES IN REBRANDED FORM (Euronews / The National, May 25; confirmed in deal-text reporting June 13):** Iran confirmed that the Strait of Hormuz will not return to pre-war operating conditions and that the Persian Gulf Strait Authority will charge "navigational service fees" for passage — explicitly framed as distinct from "tolls," which Iran acknowledges cannot be levied under international law. This is the mechanism by which Iran maintains the PGSA's institutional existence post-deal: the $2M/ship yuan/crypto toll is rebranded as a "fee for navigational services provided." Any deal that uses "no tolls" but permits "service fees" leaves the PGSA intact as a functional state institution — meaning insurance normalization, actuarial reset, and vessel willingness to return to Hormuz routes remain structurally compromised even after signing.
- **MEHR NEWS 14-POINT MOU DETAIL: MISSILE PROGRAM + PROXY SUPPORT EXCLUDED FROM SCOPE (Arab News / CNN, June 12):** The draft MOU excludes Iran's ballistic missile program and its support for regional proxy groups from the agreement's scope — these are deferred entirely to Phase 2 or subsequent negotiations. This is the most significant structural concession in the draft and Netanyahu's strongest single argument for a unilateral pre-signing strike on a nuclear facility: a signed deal leaves Iran's nuclear delivery architecture and proxy network intact, with no committed dismantlement timeline.
- **BRENT $87–90, VIX 17.68 (−9.05%), GOLD ~$4,210–4,225 (June 12–13):** Markets are pricing the Pakistan PM confirmation as near-definitive. Brent held in the deal-proximity range ($85–90) for the second consecutive session. VIX fell sharply from 19.44 to 17.68 on the Pakistan PM announcement. Gold is anomalously stable in the $4,210–4,225 range; the December 2026 Fed rate-hike expectation (from CPI 4.2% YoY) is suppressing the safe-haven bid even as signing remains unsigned.

**Gulf States Adaptation:** The Iraq al-Waleed → Syria Baniyas overland corridor remains operational; sustained Iranian ballistic escalation toward Jordan's Al-Azraq geography (June 10–11) has not yet severed it but continues to threaten the corridor's logistics chain. Saudi Arabia's east-west pipeline (~7 mb/d to Yanbu) and UAE's Habshan–Fujairah pipeline (~1.8 mb/d) continue operating unaffected by the Hormuz closure. UAE, Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, and Israel are all accelerating overland energy infrastructure and GCC Rail development as structural hedges against any future Hormuz closure. The Kirkuk–Ceyhan pipeline to Turkey's Mediterranean coast continues to carry meaningful volumes. Smaller Gulf states (Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain) without bypass infrastructure remain fully exposed to any deal-collapse scenario; larger states have priced this risk into permanent infrastructure diversification.

**Information Warfare:** Iran's dominant operation today is the "service fees" semantic rebranding: by converting the PGSA's $2M/ship yuan/crypto toll into "fees for navigational services provided," Iran achieves the same institutional outcome (IRGC retains Hormuz control mechanisms) while allowing the US to claim it blocked "tolls." This is Iran's most sophisticated information operation of the conflict — not a claim of military victory but a semantic legal architecture that embeds IRGC authority into post-deal Hormuz governance in a form the US cannot easily challenge under international law. Trump's "Khamenei personally approved" claim is a deliberate counter-operation: it publicly attributes the deal to the Supreme Leader without his acknowledgment, creating a situation where Iranian denial makes Khamenei look like he's blocking his own negotiators' work. The IRGC's internal victory narrative ("we shot at their ships and they're paying us $24B") is running on military channels while Araghchi's diplomatic track signals deal proximity — the dual-track information architecture is now more sophisticated than at any prior moment. The diaspora operation is split: pro-deal exile voices are amplifying the "$300B reconstruction for ordinary Iranians" frame, while anti-regime diaspora voices are amplifying the "Iran surrendered on nuclear weapons" frame. Iran International and Radio Farda continue being filtered domestically, meaning the regime controls which narrative reaches the Iranian public first.
