## Daily Briefing — 2026-06-25

**Overall situation:** Day 118 / Week 17. Two developments defined June 25. First, Iran's IRGC issued a direct operational warning that ships transiting Hormuz via the Oman/IMO dual-corridor framework without PGSA clearance are "unacceptable and dangerous" — the most acute single IRGC challenge to a framework backed by both the Iranian government and the US since the MOU was signed. Second, the US Senate reversed itself within 48 hours on a war powers resolution: it passed 50-48 on Tuesday (June 24) then failed Wednesday (June 25) after Trump called Cassidy a "lunatic" in a closed-door Capitol Hill confrontation, briefed him through Vance and Witkoff, and received his flip plus Paul's "present" vote. The primary binary: whether the IRGC enforces the route warning kinetically against a vessel on the IMO corridor — which would be the first direct IRGC action against a US/Iran-government-endorsed framework — or whether it is another Path C assertion undercut by the FM and CENTCOM in real time.

**Key developments:**
- **IRGC DECLARES IMO/OMAN HORMUZ CORRIDOR "UNACCEPTABLE AND DANGEROUS" (CNBC / Al Jazeera, June 25):** The IRGC specifically targeted a vessel that used the Musandam Peninsula route outlined by Oman alongside the IMO as part of the dual-corridor framework established June 23 and backed by both Iran's government and the US. The IRGC stated "the only authorized route for passage through the Strait of Hormuz is the route announced by the Islamic Republic of Iran." All vessels must contact IRGC Navy on the designated channel before transiting or face enforcement action. This creates a direct intra-Iranian contradiction: Iran's FM backed the IMO corridor six days ago; the IRGC is now operationally rejecting it. The physical reality on water is that Kpler records 4.8 million bpd flowing through Hormuz since the deal, including approximately 21 million barrels of Iranian crude exiting in June — the IRGC warning has not yet stopped ships, but creates legal ambiguity for underwriters trying to apply Lloyd's/Chubb coverage to IMO-corridor transits that PGSA has not approved.
- **SENATE PASSES THEN REVERSES WAR POWERS RESOLUTION IN 48 HOURS (ABC News / NBC News / CBS News, June 24-25):** Senate voted 50-48 on June 24 (with Cassidy, Murkowski, Collins, Paul as GOP defectors) to direct Trump to remove US forces from Iran hostilities without explicit congressional authorization. Hours later Trump called Cassidy a "lunatic" in a closed-door Senate meeting. Wednesday's late-night vote blocked the resolution from advancing: Cassidy received a White House briefing from Vance and Witkoff and voted against; Paul voted "present." Trump declared the reversal: "This vote puts Iran on notice!" The 48-hour cycle eliminates legislative constraint on Trump's Iran war authority during the 60-day window. Structural note: the 50-48 margin (with Paul now effectively abstaining) means Trump is still one determined Republican away from a binding veto-bound constraint if the deal deteriorates.
- **TRUMP: US INSPECTORS WILL JOIN IAEA IN IRAN NUCLEAR MISSIONS (The Nation / Fox News, June 25):** Trump announced in a Fox News interview that American inspectors will accompany IAEA teams when they visit Iranian uranium sites, but said "no rush" to deploy. Iran's Deputy FM immediately rejected: no meeting with Grossi was held in Switzerland; "no plan in place to grant the agency access to the attacked facilities or nuclear materials." This is the seventh principal-level contradiction of the conflict — and materially escalates the US nuclear demand beyond "IAEA access" to "US-inspector accompaniment of IAEA," a demand that Iran cannot accept without a domestic legitimacy crisis given Khamenei's May 21-22 written decree banning HEU export and Iran's constitutional framing of nuclear sovereignty. The nuclear working group at Burgenstock is now the venue for resolving a harder ask than was tabled when the working group was formed.
- **BRENT AT PRE-WAR LEVELS — $72.64–73.72/BBL (Gulf News / Bloomberg, June 25):** Brent fell more than 4% intraday on June 25, reaching its lowest level since late February and trading at essentially the same level as the day the war began ($72/bbl). Kpler data confirms 4.8 million bpd flowing through Hormuz since the deal; at least 20 stranded tankers carrying 35 million barrels total have cleared Hormuz since June 17. Iranian crude is returning to Asian markets under the TGL framework.
- **GOLD BREAKS BELOW $4,000 — DOLLAR AT 14-MONTH HIGH (Trading Economics, June 25):** Gold fell to $3,980.16 (−0.49%), the first close below $4,000 since before the Versailles deal announcement. The US dollar rose to its highest level in over a year. Fed funds futures now assign approximately 68% probability of a September rate hike, up from 29% a week ago. FOMC hawkish dot is the dominant driver; the IRGC route warning, Senate drama, and US-inspector demand produced zero safe-haven gold bid.
