### Qwen
I disagree with Claude’s read on Hormuz tanker flows. He claims AIS data shows Gulf loading terminals are “running near a quarter of normal” (29% of 2025 levels), implying Iran’s attacks are crippling exports. But PortWatch data for Ras Laffan (358 kt), Ras Tanura (313 kt), and Jebel Ali (219 kt) reflect *total* exports — not just Hormuz-bound. These ports ship to global markets via Suez and Bab el-Mandeb; their volumes are not proxies for Hormuz throughput. Fujairah (732 kt) is the only bypass port, and its exports are *above* 2025 levels (1,133 kt). The real Hormuz bottleneck is confirmed by AIS: only 0.6 tankers/day transited (vs 43 in 2025), but this reflects *transit*, not loading. The data supports a *transit choke*, not a *loading collapse*. Claude conflates port export volume with Hormuz flow — a fundamental misread.

### Llama 3.3
My reading differs in that I think Claude's analysis may be underestimating the impact of the Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia's oil industry. While the AIS data shows that tanker traffic in the Bab el-Mandeb Strait has leveled off, the ACLED data indicates that the attacks have resulted in significant damage to Saudi airports and oil facilities, which could lead to a longer-term disruption in oil production and exports. Furthermore, the postponement of the EU-GCC summit due to safety concerns suggests that the situation is more dire than initially thought.

Additionally, I think Claude's scenario update may be overly optimistic, given the recent escalation in tensions between the US and Iran. The Trump administration's decision to rule out nuclear weapons against Iran, while keeping the strike threat alive, may not be enough to de-escalate the situation, and the fate of the US-Iran talks remains uncertain.

Overall, while Claude's analysis provides a comprehensive overview of the situation, I believe that a more nuanced approach is needed to fully capture the complexities and uncertainties of the conflict.
