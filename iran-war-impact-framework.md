## Daily Briefing - 2026-11-07

**Day 253 / Week 37.** The president has now described undeclared strikes on Iran as a standing practice — "we do minor strikes" — hours after the second hit on Qeshm in five days, while CENTCOM called the same island "relatively calm." The shape of this war for the next month is an attrition loop over the strait approaches, not a three-day package, and the Red Sea data says last fortnight's tanker normalisation was a one-week artefact.

### The calls — where the read moved

**Q5 · US posture — the limited-strike policy is now confirmed by the principal, and it is a substitute for the campaign, not a prelude to it.**
Trump, Nov 6: "We do minor strikes, but they knock out. They built a little bit of an anti-aircraft device, and we knocked it out in about 15 minutes, maybe five" ([CNBC, Nov 7](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/07/trump-us-minor-strikes-iran-qeshm-island.html); [Al Jazeera, Nov 7](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/7/trump-confirms-new-us-strikes-on-irans-qeshm-island)). Tasnim/IRIB report two missiles at Suza, Qeshm; CENTCOM responded that the situation is "relatively calm" ([Hasht-e Subh, Nov 7](https://8am.media/eng/explosions-heard-in-southern-iran-us-military-says-situation-is-calm)). **This upgrades WSJ's single-originator "series of limited strikes" to presidential confirmation.** Second strike on the same island in five days, both against air defence. **Agent inference (medium-high): Iran reconstitutes SAM coverage over the transit corridor, the US removes it in minutes, neither side pays a political price — a self-sustaining loop that lowers the pressure to execute the prepared package.** 14-day probability of a major strike held at ~25%. A Diego Garcia rotation reverses it.

**Q7 · Red Sea — the tanker recovery failed its second-week test; the big crude parcels went back to the Cape.**
Week to Oct 25: Bab el-Mandeb **372k tanker DWT/day, −33% w/w and −49% vs 2025**, against 554k and −5% the week before (PortWatch). **Arithmetic: tanker count barely moved, 10.0 → 9.6, so average parcel size fell 55.4k → 38.8k DWT, −30%. Suez carries the identical signature — count up 15.9 → 16.3, tonnage down 841k → 660k, average −23%.** Two chokepoints, one mechanism: VLCC/Suezmax cargoes withdrew, products kept running. The structural case is consistent — the Houthis told Riyadh an exit costs **$51bn plus recognition** ([WSJ, Nov 5](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/saudi-arabia-has-no-easy-choices-as-it-weighs-how-to-combat-houthi-threat-a4adc358)). Confidence medium-high. A single week back above 500k would reopen it.

**Q1 · Flows — Iran is converting a blanket closure into a permission regime, and the attack line has moved inside the Gulf.**
Tehran is reported to be exempting India, China, Qatar and Iraq from its Hormuz restrictions ([Al Jazeera, Nov 6](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/6/why-is-iran-exempting-india-china-qatar-iraq-from-hormuz-restrictions)) and has reshuffled its transit blacklist; the *Velos Sapphire* was struck ~50nm north of Qatar on a **Fujairah→Sitra** leg, one seafarer missing ([Splash247, Nov 6](https://splash247.com/seafarer-missing-in-gulf-tanker-strike-as-iran-redraws-hormuz-blacklist)). **Read: the premium is migrating from the chokepoint onto the intra-Gulf shuttle leg that the whole export bridge depends on — a far more expensive place for it to sit.** Against that, four Arab producers exported **15.53 mb/d in October, +10% m/m** ([BigGo citing Kpler/Vortexa, Nov 3](https://finance.biggo.com/news/a2065723-a9b5-462b-8b35-be70bc48f1e2)) while AIS shows 6k DWT/day at the strait. Confidence medium.
