## Daily Briefing — 2026-07-13

**Overall situation:** Day 136 / Week 20. Trump formally announced the US is "reinstating" the naval blockade of Iran — crossing from threat to policy — while CENTCOM completed a new round of strikes deploying one-way attack sea drones for the first time. Brent gapped to $79.12 on Monday open (+4.2%), the first market reading of Sunday's Iran-strikes-six-Gulf-states escalation. Hormuz crossings are at near-standstill; Iran and the US are now publicly disputing whether the strait is "open," a new information-warfare front. The diplomatic channel is not dead — Qatari mediators traveled to Tehran and a Switzerland round is tentatively expected next week — but the blockade reinstatement shrinks that window substantially.

**Key developments:**
- **TRUMP FORMALLY REINSTATES IRAN NAVAL BLOCKADE** ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/liveblog/2026/7/13/iran-war-live-us-bombs-iranian-cities-again-as-hormuz-standoff-intensifies), July 13): Trump declared the US is "reinstating" the Iran naval blockade and said the US "will charge ships" for guarding the Strait of Hormuz — the first time he has framed the blockade as a revenue mechanism rather than a purely military action. The previous blockade was lifted June 18 under the Islamabad MOU. Reinstatement returns the conflict to its April–June kinetic peak, with Iran previously responding to the blockade by mining approaches and striking Gulf bases. Trump also said the US would "get paid a lot of money" for guarding the waterway — a novel framing that reframes occupation as commerce.
- **CENTCOM DEPLOYS ONE-WAY ATTACK SEA DRONES FOR FIRST TIME** ([OAN](https://www.oann.com/newsroom/centcom-forces-complete-another-round-of-strikes-against-iran-deploy-one-way-attack-sea-drones-for-the-first-time/), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-13/us-and-iran-trade-fresh-strikes-dispute-whether-hormuz-is-open), July 13): CENTCOM completed another round of strikes against Iran — targeting air-defense systems, coastal radar sites, missile and drone capabilities, and small boats — using fighter aircraft, naval vessels, one-way aerial drones, and for the first time, one-way attack sea drones. This represents a new weapons category in the theater. Iran and the US issued conflicting accounts of whether the Strait remains open; CENTCOM characterized the campaign as degrading Iran's ability to attack civilian mariners.
- **BRENT GAPS TO $79.12 ON MONDAY OPEN — +4.2%** ([Al Jazeera Economy](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/7/13/oil-prices-jump-as-us-and-iran-trade-attacks-over-strait-of-hormuz), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/13/us-iran-war-hormuz-oil-trump.html), July 13): Brent crude surged from a Friday close of $76.01 to $79.12 at Monday open (range $77.82–$79.39), the first market reading of Sunday's escalation. This was slightly below the $82–88 prediction in yesterday's briefing; Fujairah terminal not reporting damage at time of writing is the primary reason the ceiling held. The ~$79 level still doesn't fully price blockade reinstatement, which was announced after Monday trading commenced.
- **M/V GFS GALAXY — ONE CREW MEMBER MISSING, SHIP DISABLED** ([World Cargo News](https://www.worldcargonews.com/news/2026/07/container-ship-gfs-galaxy-heavily-damaged-in-strait-of-hormuz-attack-one-crew-member-missing/), [Defcon Level](https://www.defconlevel.com/alert/2026-07-11-u-s-central-central-command-launches-strikes-in-response-to-attack-on-m-v-gfs-galaxy-in-strait-of-hormuz-iran-announces-closure-report), July 11): The Cyprus-flagged container ship M/V GFS Galaxy was attacked by IRGC forces at 22:40 UTC on July 11 while transiting eastbound through the Strait after departing Jebel Ali, suffering engine-room damage and onboard fire; crew abandoned ship in lifeboats. One crew member remains missing. This attack directly triggered CENTCOM's strike response and Iran's formal Strait closure declaration. Iran's account: it fired a "warning shot" at a vessel that had deactivated its tracking system and strayed from approved routes.
- **QATAR MEDIATORS TRAVEL TO TEHRAN — SWITZERLAND ROUND POSSIBLE NEXT WEEK** ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/07/10/trump-iran-talks-ceasefire-over), [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/10/world/live-news/iran-war-trump), [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/iran/trump-us-agreed-iran-request-continue-talks-ceasefire-rcna385833), July 10–13): Despite Trump declaring the ceasefire "over," Qatari negotiators traveled to Iran to meet officials in a de-escalation bid. Trump said the US "agreed to Iran's request to continue talks" — a diplomatic opening that coexists with the blockade announcement. A next Switzerland round is tentatively signaled for the coming week. The ceasefire framework is dead; whether new talks can produce a fresh framework is the open question.

**On the Ground in Iran:** Large multi-city protests continued July 12 across Tehran, Shush, Ahvaz, Dezful, Kermanshah, Rasht, Karaj, and Tabriz, with Social Security retirees, plundered depositors, and workers protesting poverty, inflation, and systemic economic mismanagement (NCRI, July 12). The June 2026 tally was 135+ separate protests, strikes, and demonstrations — the highest monthly count since the war began. Domestic fuel crisis remains structural. IRGC posture: despite CENTCOM's sustained degradation campaign (300+ targets over three nights), Iran demonstrated significant redundancy — continuing Gulf state strikes within hours of each US wave. The new-weapons deployment (sea drones) signals US assessment that Iranian coastal small-boat and radar networks remain operational.

**Gulf States Adaptation:** Fujairah terminal: no damage reported as of Monday morning — the critical threshold from yesterday's briefing was not crossed, which explains why Brent only gapped to $79 rather than $85+. Saudi east-west pipeline (7M bbl/day to Yanbu) and UAE Habshan–Fujairah pipeline (1.8M bbl/day) remain the active bypass infrastructure. UAE pipeline expansion announced May 15 (ADNOC's "West-East 1") will double Fujairah export capacity but is not operational until 2027. IRGC's ongoing strikes on Gulf states — including Oman and UAE — now directly threaten the physical security of the bypass infrastructure. Iran's IRGC claimed it destroyed radar systems in Oman's Musandam Governorate (which flanks the Strait entrance) — if confirmed, this degrades Oman's ability to monitor Iran's naval activity near the chokepoint. Qatar: Al Udeid Air Base struck again; Qatar simultaneously serving as bombing target and mediating party — its leverage to demand direct Iran-US talks has never been higher.

**Sovereign Debt Stress:** Jordan faces its highest political instability since the war began — three Iranian missile strikes on Jordanian territory in two days, with the kingdom caught between its US alignment and the need for regional neutrality. Jordan's economy depends on Gulf remittances and the US security umbrella; the strikes create domestic political pressure without giving Amman retaliatory options. Egypt: Suez Canal revenue collapse continues as Hormuz closure eliminates the LNG/crude transits that would otherwise route via Cape → Suez. Pakistan: S&P flagged Pakistan as facing the highest macro-financial stress risk among Asia-Pacific economies; refinancing needs average $24B/year 2026–2030. Pakistan also holds the weakest geopolitical hand — it organized the Islamabad MOU, which has now collapsed. IMF: no new emergency action reported; combined Egypt/Pakistan/Kenya debt maturities of $5.1B in Nov-Dec 2026 represent the next hard deadline.

**Reconstruction Race:** No new China or Russia reconstruction announcements today. The structural baseline: Wang Yi's June 22 pledge remains the largest firm reconstruction commitment on the table; each CENTCOM strike wave expands Iran's reconstruction needs and China's leverage. COSCO's bonded crude storage accumulation at Zhoushan/Ningbo (flagged in yesterday's playbook) is the quiet move to watch. Russia continues to benefit from Trump's Russian oil sanctions waiver — an economic windfall that rewards Moscow's restraint. The US-Iran information dispute over whether Hormuz is "open" creates a parallel reconstruction-narrative battle: China's UNSC humanitarian corridor gambit (from yesterday's playbook) would be a natural next move.

**Information Warfare:** Two parallel narrative battles today: (1) US vs. Iran over whether the Strait is "open" — CENTCOM says it is degrading Iran's ability to attack ships; Iran says it has formally closed the Strait. Each side is targeting different audiences: CENTCOM speaks to insurers and shippers (trying to hold down war-risk premiums and encourage transit); Iran speaks to regional states (demonstrating control). (2) Trump's "will charge ships" framing — rebranding the blockade as a protection-for-hire arrangement rather than a military action — is a domestic political move (makes the blockade revenue-generating rather than cost-incurring) but internationally reads as extortion, handing Iran a narrative gift with Global South audiences. Qatar's dual status (bombing target + mediator) is the most under-reported leverage point in the conflict; any Qatari ultimatum on continued US base access at Al Udeid would immediately reframe the information battle.
