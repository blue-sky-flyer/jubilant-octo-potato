## Daily Briefing - 2026-11-12

**Day 258 / Week 37.** Two numbers arrived today that price the "third option": Iran is assessed to be building about 50 ballistic missiles a month, and the land half of the blockade is being refused by the three countries that would have to run it. The strategy of indefinite strangulation now has a visible monthly cost and a visible leak.

### The calls — where the read moved

**Q5 · US posture — the status quo is not static: it accrues roughly 50 ballistic missiles a month to the other side.**
A Western official citing military intelligence tells Bloomberg that Iran's military-industrial capacity is at **~50% of pre-war**, producing **~50 ballistic missiles per month**, with **at least 1,700 remaining** plus possibly thousands of Shahed-type weapons ([JPost, Nov 12](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/iran-news/article-914657)). Alma independently puts the Feb 28 stock at ~2,500 and today's at 1,700, with ~650 fired at Israel ([Alma, Nov 12](https://israel-alma.org/irans-missile-arsenal-erosion-rebuilding-renewed-erosion-and-a-further-attempt-to-rebuild)). **Agent arithmetic: 2,500 − 650 = 1,850 expected; 1,700 observed, so strikes net of production removed only ~150. At 50/month over 8.5 months (~425 built), ~575 were destroyed on the ground — against the ~4,500 targets the IDF says it struck in Iran.** With the air campaign dormant, the stockpile now grows. **This also retires Trump's Nov 8 "10% of missile capacity" claim as unsupportable.** Confidence medium. A dated strike on assembly or solid-fuel production breaks it.

**Q6 · Israel — the binding constraint is no longer the election calendar, it is who may lawfully authorise a war.**
Israel's Security Cabinet convenes Sunday for the first time since **Oct 5** — and had instructed the IDF to raise readiness for a possible Iran strike without having met, with measures that "could lead to military activity that risks triggering a broader war." Eisenkot, tasked with forming a government, has demanded Netanyahu refrain from unnecessary operations until a new government exists and asked the attorney-general to guarantee him briefings; Netanyahu calls the move irresponsible ([JPost, Nov 12](https://www.jpost.com/israel-election-2026/article-914671)). Against it: Home Front logs three hostile alerts on Nov 8 and nothing hostile since (Home Front alerts). **Agent inference (medium): a caretaker premier contesting strike authority with his designated successor through the AG lowers the odds of an Israeli-initiated opening move during the transition.** Confidence medium. A published post-Sunday cabinet authorisation, or a funded reservist call-up, reverses it.

**Q2 · Inside Iran — Iron Gate is being refused rather than implemented, and the Caspian is going dark.**
The US push to tighten the blockade is meeting "significant obstacles" from Turkey, Iraq and Pakistan, which fear economic damage and civil unrest; Iraq has refused to close most crossings ([Ynet, Nov 12](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/skesremcfg)). Iraq's foreign minister confirms Treasury asked for all but two closed and says talks continue ([JPost, Nov 9](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-914393)); Shaib reopened Nov 10. **The measurement consequence is the bigger item: approaches to Anzali, Nowshahr, Amirabad and the Russian Caspian ports are now devoid of AIS signals** ([Maritime Executive, Nov 8](https://maritime-executive.com/article/centcom-mirrors-naval-blockade-with-iron-gate-for-iran-s-borders)). **Agent inference (medium-high): from the next print, Caspian call counts become a floor like Hormuz, not a measure — do not read a Caspian decline as a trade decline.** Confidence high on the resistance. A dated Iraqi closure order beyond two crossings moves it.

1. **Caspian AIS blackout significance**: While Claude reads the AIS disappearance as a blockade-evasion tactic (PortWatch/MarEx), I assess it more likely reflects Russian-Iranian coordination to conceal arms shipments specifically, not general trade. This distinction matters for monitoring sanctions enforcement.

2. **Israeli strike calculus**: Claude weights political constraints heavily, but IDF Chief Zamir's pre-election blockade of strikes (Ynet Nov 6) suggests operational readiness may be the tighter bottleneck. The 1,700-missile stockpile (Alma Nov 12) reduces urgency for preemptive action, as Israel can absorb ~550 more strikes at the 2025-26 launch rate before reaching the "unacceptable damage" threshold that triggered Roaring Lion.

3. **Hormuz negotiation postures**: Claude's "Ghalibaf over Rezaei" weighting underplays the significance of Rezaei's threat to European bases (Iran Intl Nov 11). ### Qwen
I largely agree with Claude’s read. One genuine difference:  

Claude treats the 50 missiles/month production rate as a credible, stable baseline. But the *only* source is a single anonymous Western official cited by Bloomberg, with no corroboration from Alma (which infers stockpile changes from Israeli strike claims, not production rates). The 1,700 stockpile estimate also relies on the same unverified source. Meanwhile, Iran’s Caspian trade routes are visibly shifting to AIS-dark operations — suggesting covert, non-commercial logistics. This implies the 50/month figure may reflect *reconstituted* production from hidden, dispersed facilities, not a steady industrial output. I would lower confidence in the 50/month as a *predictive* metric to *low*, not medium. The real trend is not production volume, but resilience through concealment — a strategic shift, not a recovery.
