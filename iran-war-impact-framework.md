**Key developments:**
- **US TREASURY ISSUES 60-DAY OIL SANCTIONS WAIVER (TGL) — IRANIAN CRUDE AND US REFINERIES AUTHORIZED (Treasury / Express Tribune / IndexBox, June 22–23):** Treasury Secretary Bessent issued a Temporary General License authorizing the production, delivery, and sale of Iranian crude oil, petrochemicals, and petroleum products for 60 days through August 21. The TGL also explicitly permits US refineries to import Iranian crude — a reversal of 40+ years of strict prohibition. Issued post-Burgenstock as a direct implementation step. Critical structural observation: the TGL's August 21 expiry is five days after the MOU's 60-day PGSA toll suspension (August 16), creating a dual-expiry cliff: if Phase 2 nuclear talks don't produce a framework by mid-August, both PGSA fees and oil sanctions return within days of each other, giving Iran a structural incentive to stretch Phase 2 talks toward August rather than close early.
- **IRAN FM BAGHAEI DENIES IAEA INSPECTORS-RETURN AGREEMENT — DIRECT CONTRADICTION OF VANCE (NBC News / ABC News / CBS News, June 23):** Baghaei stated Iran has "no plans" to allow IAEA inspectors access to nuclear sites damaged during the war, directly contradicting Vance's June 22 confirmation that Iran agreed and his expectation that IAEA would arrive "this week." The denial is specifically calibrated to "war-damaged sites" — leaving the door open to inspectors at undamaged enrichment facilities, which is likely what Vance described. Fox News reporting simultaneously confirmed inspectors are being received, with caveats about access limitations. The IAEA's actual arrival or denial at functional nuclear sites this week is the primary nuclear-track binary of the period.
- **FOUR QATARI LNG TANKERS ENTERING HORMUZ — FIRST SINCE MARCH FORCE MAJEURE (Reuters / CNBC, June 22–23):** Four QatarEnergy LNG carriers — Wadi Al Sail, Mekaines, Al Sadd, and Mesaimeer — entered the Strait via the PGSA-mandated northern route (Larak Island corridor), the first Qatari LNG movement since QatarEnergy declared force majeure on all LNG contracts in late March. QatarEnergy has not formally reversed force majeure; this is an operational test. If all four transit successfully, the corporate force majeure reversal becomes the next strategic chokepoint for European LNG supply recovery — a QatarEnergy board decision that cannot logically be sustained once vessels are demonstrably transiting. Also transiting: five vessels including three Saudi VLCCs carrying approximately 6M barrels of Saudi crude on June 22.
- **PEZESHKIAN TRAVELS TO PAKISTAN — HIGHEST-LEVEL DIPLOMATIC FOLLOW-ON TO BURGENSTOCK (Al Jazeera, June 23):** Iranian President Pezeshkian is traveling to Pakistan directly following the Burgenstock talks, the first presidential foreign travel since the Versailles signing. Pakistan has been Iran's primary interlocutor throughout the conflict. Pezeshkian's visit almost certainly involves a private channel to communicate Iran's actual IAEA position — distinct from Baghaei's public denial — allowing Pakistan to relay to Washington what Iran can accept versus what it must deny to domestic audiences. The visit is the most operationally significant back-channel signal of the day.
- **LEBANON DE-CONFLICTION CELL'S FIRST OPERATIONAL TEST — IDF FIRES NEAR NABATIEH, 2 KILLED (Times of Israel liveblog, June 23):** IDF struck a group near Nabatieh in southern Lebanon on the cell's first full day; Israel said it targeted Hezbollah gunmen threatening forces at Ali Taher ridge; Hezbollah claimed IDF killed civilians and declared a "blatant ceasefire violation." Two killed. Lebanon's 5th round of political and military talks is meeting June 23–24. Whether the de-confliction cell produces a de-escalatory communication before Hezbollah retaliates is the immediate operational test of the mechanism created at Burgenstock.
- **TECHNICAL WORKING GROUPS ACTIVE AT BURGENSTOCK WITHOUT PRINCIPALS (STL News / SpecialEurasia, June 23):** Specialized working groups on nuclear, sanctions, and monitoring/dispute-resolution are meeting at the Burgenstock resort following the departure of Vance and Ghalibaf. The nuclear group is the venue where the IAEA access dispute will either be resolved or formally deadlocked. The monitoring group is establishing the compliance metrics that will trigger the $24B installment releases. The sanctions group is operationalizing the TGL's 60-day window into a longer-term sanctions-relief architecture.
