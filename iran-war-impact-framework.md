## Daily Briefing — 2026-05-24

**Overall situation:** Day 86 / Week 13. The most consequential diplomatic development of the war: Trump declared a deal with Iran is "largely negotiated" on Saturday, with the announcement expected as soon as Sunday afternoon. The framework is a two-phase structure — Phase 1 delivers Hormuz reopening and oil sanctions relief; Phase 2 defers nuclear negotiations to a 30–60-day window. The deal is not yet signed ("50-50" in Trump's own words to Axios), and the critical nuclear question — HEU stockpile disposal — remains explicitly deferred to Phase 2. The binary for the next 24 hours is whether Trump and Tehran confirm the draft agreed Saturday or whether the announcement falls apart before it can be made.

**Key developments:**
- **TRUMP: DEAL "LARGELY NEGOTIATED," ANNOUNCEMENT EXPECTED SUNDAY** — Trump told reporters on Saturday the deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz is "largely negotiated" and could be announced by Sunday. Axios (May 24, confirmed 2026 URL) reports the framework has two phases: Phase 1 = Iran clears mines and reopens Hormuz with no tolls; US lifts naval blockade of Iranian ports and issues oil sanctions waivers. Phase 2 = 30–60-day negotiations covering HEU stockpile disposal and enrichment moratorium terms, both left to later determination. The deal framework has been approved by top negotiators on both sides: Vance, Witkoff, and Kushner for the US; Iranian Parliament Speaker Ghalibaf (the pragmatist track figure that ISW-CTP had sidelined) for Iran. Trump told Axios it is "50-50" and said he will have a decision by Sunday. (Axios, CNN, NPR, CNBC, Fortune — all May 24, 2026)
- **GHALIBAF APPROVES DRAFT — PRAGMATIST TRACK REASSERTS ITSELF** — The inclusion of Ghalibaf as the Iranian signatory is structurally significant: ISW-CTP had assessed Vahidi and the IRGC military track as dominant over Ghalibaf's pragmatist faction since at least April 26. If Ghalibaf has approved the draft, it means either the Supreme Leader has rebalanced toward the pragmatist track or the IRGC accepted the Phase 2 nuclear deferral as sufficient cover to proceed. (Axios May 24, 2026)
- **TIMES OF ISRAEL: TRUMP TOLD NETANYAHU FINAL DEAL WILL SEE NUCLEAR PROGRAM DISMANTLED, ALL HEU REMOVED** — An Israeli official says Trump personally briefed Netanyahu that the final deal — meaning the end-state of Phase 2 — will include full nuclear program dismantlement and all enriched uranium removed from Iran. Trump's call with Netanyahu was described as going "very well." This is either a genuine Israeli red-line assurance that unlocks Israeli tacit approval for Phase 1 signing, or it is Israeli spin designed to set a Phase 2 standard that could later be invoked to block any deal that falls short. (Times of Israel, May 24, 2026)
- **UK PM STARMER ENDORSES DEAL STRUCTURE** — Prime Minister Keir Starmer said any agreement must include "unrestricted freedom of navigation" through the Strait of Hormuz, while explicitly welcoming progress toward a deal. UK endorsement extends the coalition of deal-supportive parties to include a major NATO ally for the first time. (Starmer statement, May 24, 2026)
- **BRENT RISES TO $106 INTRADAY** — Oil futures climbed to as high as $106/bbl early Sunday on deal-proximity optimism, above the Friday close of $103.54. (Trading data, May 24, 2026)

**On the Ground in Iran:** The internet blackout enters Day 86 — over 2,100 hours, still the longest continuous national shutdown in documented modern history per NetBlocks. ⚠️ Iran International and IranWire report dairy prices rising 20% effective June 1, with rice prices already tripled year-on-year; fuel rationing is spreading from major cities to provincial centers. A state newspaper admitted "caloric poverty" is accelerating, with families unable to afford basic staples. If the deal framework signed in the next 24 hours, the domestic economic relief pathway would open only after Hormuz physically reopens (Phase 1) and before the nuclear Phase 2 creates new uncertainty — a narrow window for the regime to claim a material civilian benefit from the settlement. ⚠️ The internet blackout itself is not part of the disclosed deal terms and is likely to persist regardless of Hormuz status.

**Gulf States Adaptation:** Qatar and Pakistan are the primary deal architects of the moment — the Qatari delegation that arrived in Tehran on Saturday alongside Munir's Pakistan Army Chief presence represents the highest simultaneous concentration of Gulf-linked mediators in Tehran of the conflict. AGBI (May 2026) confirmed trucking volumes across the Saudi-Oman border have surged to record levels, with TruKKer now picking up hundreds of loads daily from Khor Fakkan and land bridges active across Fujairah, Sohar, Duqm, Salalah (Oman), Jeddah, Yanbu, and Neom (Saudi Arabia). A deal-driven Hormuz reopening would reduce but not immediately end the overland route investment, as war-risk insurance normalization will lag the political agreement by weeks to months.

**Information Warfare:** The deal framework announcement changes the information warfare landscape if it holds: Iran would claim a victory narrative (no immediate HEU transfer, oil sanctions lifted, blockade ended, nuclear sovereignty preserved into Phase 2 talks) while the US claims it achieved Hormuz reopening and nuclear commitments deferred to structured talks.
