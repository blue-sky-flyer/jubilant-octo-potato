NATO's collective-defense response is confirmed by CNN. If Iran were to strike US bases in Europe, NATO's Article 5 collective-defense obligation would activate — a qualitatively different escalation ladder than the existing six-country Gulf footprint.

4. **Hormuz routing shift confirmed: 80%+ of transits now using Omani corridor or AIS-dark; Iran "has lost significant control" per Kpler data** ([CNN](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=16lV63DiCa8), Aug 19 — double-sourced against UANI Aug 19 Omani-route framing): CNN's Aug 19 analysis, citing Kpler ship-tracking data, confirms that more than 80% of liquids transits through Hormuz over the past two weeks have taken the Omani/UN-authorized corridor or AIS-dark routes — despite Iran's stated opposition to the Omani route. Iran says the Strait "will stay closed until the US meets certain conditions." Trump says "significant volumes of oil were still passing through Hormuz" ([TradingEconomics](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/brent-crude-oil), Aug 20 — Trump claim, not independently verified as a precise figure). The Kpler routing data is the most current quantitative Hormuz transit picture in the pack and supersedes the prior IMF PortWatch 1% figure (which measured commercial transit at the strictest definition). The two readings are not contradictory: 80%+ via Omani/dark routes means ships are moving but not through Iran-controlled channels — Iran's enforcement has displaced routing rather than stopped volume entirely. This is analytically significant for Brent pricing: if 80% of pre-crisis volume is transiting via alternative routes, the physical supply disruption is less severe than the 1% PortWatch figure implies, which partially explains the $91–93 Brent range rather than $100+.
